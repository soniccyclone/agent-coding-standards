# Information arriving from outside your model can be squeezed into an ordinary value; effects leaving it cannot

**Lesson:** Every model of a system draws a boundary and describes what is inside it. Interactions with the outside come in two directions and they are not symmetric, which is worth knowing before you design the vocabulary. Something that brings information in still fits the ordinary shape: it produces a value that gets stored somewhere inside, and the only unusual thing about it is that its inputs are not internal state, so the model has nothing to say about why it produced what it did. That is a gap in explanatory power, not in the notation — you can write it down with the constructs you already have, and the model stays consistent.

Something that sends information out does not fit at all. Its point is a change that happens beyond the boundary, so it yields nothing to store and there is no internal state that differs afterwards. Trying to express it with the value-producing vocabulary forces you to invent a fake result and a place to put it, which is a lie in the model — it claims an internal change occurred where none did, and it makes the outbound effect look like ordinary computation that a reader could reason about locally. The honest response is a separate category with its own construct, whose entire meaning is that something is being done whose consequence the model does not track.

The payoff for the separation is that the boundary becomes readable off the text. Every occurrence of the effect construct is a place where the artifact touches something it does not describe, and that set is exactly what you need for a whole family of questions: what is not reversible, what is not repeatable, what is not testable in isolation, what has to be stubbed, what makes two runs differ. When effects are notated identically to computations, that set is not recoverable without reading everything and knowing which named operations happen to have consequences. When they have their own construct, the answer is a search.

The general instruction is to make the distinction structural rather than by convention. Naming discipline — calling the effectful ones something recognizable — degrades the moment someone is in a hurry, and it cannot be checked. A distinct construct can be checked, can be counted, and can be forbidden in regions where it must not appear. The asymmetry with inbound information is worth remembering too, since it explains why "read the outside world" so often escapes the same scrutiny: it looks like a normal computation because it can be written as one, and so it accumulates unnoticed in places where the model implicitly promised determinism.

**Source:** [Structure and Interpretation of Computer Programs](../works/structure-and-interpretation-of-computer-programs.md) — chapter 5 section 5.1.1, the "Actions" discussion accompanying the machine extended to read its inputs and print its result: the observation that the reading operation is like the others in producing a value that can be stored in a register but takes its inputs from no register, its value depending on something happening outside the parts of the machine being designed, and is therefore drawn and notated like any other value-producing operation; contrasted with the printing operation, which is said to differ in a fundamental way because it produces no output value to be stored and its effect is not on any part of the machine being designed, is given the separate name "action", and requires a new controller instruction whose meaning is to make the action happen rather than to assign anything; together with the footnote conceding that treating reading and printing as primitives glosses over a great deal of complexity, since a large portion of a real language implementation is dedicated to making them work.
