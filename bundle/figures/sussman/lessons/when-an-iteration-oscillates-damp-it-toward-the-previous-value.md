# When an iteration oscillates instead of converging, damp the step rather than abandoning the formulation

**Lesson:** A perfectly correct restatement of a problem can produce a procedure that never terminates. Seeking a square root as a fixed point of dividing the input by the guess is mathematically impeccable and computationally useless: each guess maps to the next, the next maps back to the first, and the search oscillates around the answer forever. The formulation is right and the iteration is unusable, a combination worth recognizing because the instinct is to conclude the approach was wrong.

The repair is small and general. The answer lies between the current guess and the value the transformation produces, so instead of jumping all the way, move partway by averaging the two. Oscillation is the symptom of steps that overshoot, and halving the step converts divergence into convergence without touching the underlying formulation at all. Naming the technique separately, rather than burying it in the square-root code, is what lets it be reused: the same damping makes cube roots converge, and higher roots need it applied more than once.

The most instructive part comes at the end. Once damping is applied, the sequence of approximations is *precisely the same* as the one produced by a square-root procedure written much earlier from an entirely different starting point. Two derivations sharing no visible reasoning arrive at one identical process. Treat that as a general signal: when an ad hoc procedure someone wrote by intuition coincides with an instance of a general method, it was never ad hoc but an unrecognized special case, and recognizing it retroactively supplies the justification the original never had, plus every generalization the general method already carries.

The habit, then: when an iterative scheme misbehaves, ask whether the step size rather than the formulation is at fault; and when two procedures produce identical sequences, go looking for the general method they are both instances of.

**Source:** [Structure and Interpretation of Computer Programs](../works/structure-and-interpretation-of-computer-programs.md) - chapter 1 sections 1.3.3 and 1.3.4, where the fixed-point search for y mapping to x/y is shown not to converge because successive guesses reproduce each other, average damping is introduced as averaging the guess with the transformed guess to control the oscillation, and the resulting sequence is noted to be precisely the same as the one generated by the original square-root procedure of section 1.1.7 -- with damping later abstracted into its own higher-order procedure and reused for cube roots.
