# A convenient notation is a compression that presupposes a restriction, so work in the ugly explicit form until you know what the surface must express

**Lesson:** Nested expressions are already a network of places joined by producers — every intermediate result of a subexpression occupies a place, it is just an anonymous and transient one. What the familiar notation contributes on top of that is a compression: because each of those anonymous places has exactly one thing that fills it and exactly one thing that reads it, position in the tree is enough to identify it, and no name is needed. The compression is worth having and it is also the reason the underlying restriction is unthinkable from inside the notation. You cannot write down a place fed by two producers in a language where places are identified by where they sit in a tree, so the restriction never comes up for discussion; it is not defended, it is presupposed by the writing system. Any convenient notation should be suspected of doing this. Ask what its compression depends on, because whatever that is, you will not be able to consider changing it while continuing to write in it.

The consequence for method is that exploring a model whose restriction you have lifted requires dropping to a form where every place is named and every connection is made by hand, and that form will be genuinely unpleasant — closer in feel to assembly language than to anything you would want to write programs in. The discipline is to accept that and keep two questions apart: whether the model can express what you hoped, and whether the surface is tolerable. Answer the first in the unpleasant notation and defer the second, even at the cost of publishing examples you have to apologize for. The temptation runs the other way, because a nice surface makes the work presentable, but a surface designed before the model's range is known will be shaped around the cases you had already thought of, and it will then conceal the rest exactly as the original notation concealed the restriction you just lifted. You would have built a new prison with a better view.

There is a constructive half, though, and it is the reason deferral is not just stoicism. Writing in the explicit form makes the recurring shapes visible, and those shapes are what the eventual surface should be made of rather than what you would have guessed. Insisting that something be true of a place turns out to be nothing more than installing a producer of a fixed value, and forbidding something is the same move with the opposite value, because the machinery that detects conflict already exists; a mutual-distinctness requirement is a fold of the two-place case; an n-way choice is a chain of two-way choices. Each of these is a small abbreviation over the raw form rather than a new mechanism, and their accumulation is a notation that grew out of what the model actually does. That is the honest order: mechanism, then observed idiom, then notation — never notation first, because a notation invented ahead of the idioms is a guess about which programs people will want to write.

**Source:** [The Art of the Propagator](../works/the-art-of-the-propagator.md) — the introduction's enumeration of places, which includes the implicit transient storage given to the results of subexpressions; the authors' repeated characterization of their own wiring notation as ugly and more analogous to assembly language than to the expression languages we are used to, together with the footnote sketching what an expression-oriented frontend could make of the occupancy puzzle without building one; and the ambiguity-utilities passage, where requiring and forbidding a condition reduce to writing a fixed value into a place because conflict detection is already present, distinctness is the pairwise case folded over a list, and an n-way choice is assembled as a chain of binary ones.
