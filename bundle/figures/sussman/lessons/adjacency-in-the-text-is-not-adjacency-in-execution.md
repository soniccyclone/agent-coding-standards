# Adjacency in the text is not adjacency in execution, and machinery that conflates them computes false summaries

**Lesson:** Once you attach summaries to pieces of a system and derive a composite's summary from its parts, the derivation rule depends entirely on how the parts relate. Two things that run one after the other compose one way: whatever the second requires is required of the whole unless the first supplies it. Two things that are alternatives compose differently: both sets of requirements are live, because either branch may be the one taken and nothing the other branch does can be relied upon. And a thing merely stored next to another, to be entered later from somewhere else entirely, composes in a third way: it contributes nothing to the requirements of what it sits beside, because control never flows from one to the other at all.

Conflating these is easy because they look identical in the artifact. The pieces end up laid out one after another regardless, so the natural implementation is a single combining operation that concatenates and merges. Using it where the relation is not sequential does not corrupt the output's contents — the instructions are still in the right places, the program still runs — it corrupts the summaries, and the summaries are what downstream decisions are made from. A branch arm's assignments get treated as satisfying a later requirement that only one of the two arms actually satisfies. A stored body's needs get charged to the code it merely sits behind. The optimizer then omits protection that was necessary, or adds protection that was not, and the failure surfaces far from the mistaken merge.

The remedy is to have as many combining operations as you have relations, named for the relation rather than for the layout: one for sequential execution, one for mutually exclusive alternatives, one for "this is here for convenience and is entered from elsewhere." Each computes the composite's summary correctly for its relation. They may all concatenate identically; that is not an argument for unifying them, because their contents-behaviour is the part that coincides and their summary-behaviour is the part that matters. Naming them separately also documents, at every use site, which relation the author believed held — which is exactly the fact a reader cannot recover from the concatenated result.

The general principle is that whenever you derive a property of a whole from properties of its parts, the derivation is a function of the composition relation, and any composition operator that is agnostic about the relation is quietly assuming one of them. The place to look for this bug in any system is wherever "put these together" exists as a single operation while the things being put together stand in more than one kind of relationship — plan steps that may or may not run, configuration fragments that override versus merge, effects on paths that are alternatives rather than successors.

**Source:** [Structure and Interpretation of Computer Programs](../works/structure-and-interpretation-of-computer-programs.md) — chapter 5 sections 5.5.2 and 5.5.3: the compilation of conditionals, where the code for the true and false branches is combined with a distinct combiner rather than the ordinary sequential one, explicitly because the two branches are not executed sequentially; the compilation of lambda expressions, which uses yet another distinct combiner to attach the procedure body to the procedure-construction code, explicitly because the body is not part of the sequence of instructions that will run when the combined sequence is entered but is there only because that was a convenient place to put it; the same distinct alternatives-combiner reappearing for the primitive and compiled branches of a procedure call, for the same stated reason; and section 5.5.4's derivation rule for the sequential combiner, which specifies that the composite modifies whatever either part modifies and needs the first part's needs together with those of the second that the first does not itself supply — a rule that is correct only under sequential execution.
