# Represent a choice as a manufactured assumption rather than performing it, and undo becomes reasoning you already have instead of machinery you have to build

**Lesson:** A decision taken during a computation is normally an event: one alternative is selected, the computation proceeds from it, and the other alternative ceases to exist. Making that decision revocable then requires machinery invented for the purpose — a copy of the state to return to, a stack of pending alternatives, an unwinding path — machinery which is separate from what the rest of the system does and comes with its own bookkeeping and its own defects. The alternative is not to take the decision at all. Manufacture a fresh token whose meaning is nothing more than "the first alternative is the one," manufacture a second token for the other alternative, and assert both alternatives at once, each one holding only relative to its own token. Then add one small rule that keeps any settled state believing exactly one token of the pair. Nothing has been chosen and nothing has been destroyed; what exists is a pair of claims, each qualified by a synthesized assumption.

The payoff is that every capability the system already has for handling evidence now applies to decisions, and none of it had to be extended. A conclusion automatically records which choices it depended on, because the token sits in its grounds indistinguishably from an ordinary premise. Reversing a choice requires no saved state, since the other alternative was never removed and merely awaits its token being believed. A discovery that some collection of assumptions cannot be held together prunes decisions and observations by the same rule, because nothing in the machinery can tell a manufactured token from a real premise. And one operation becomes available that has no counterpart at all in the event-based framing: when both tokens of a single choice are individually blocked, the reasons against each can be combined into a statement about the *other* assumptions involved — one that mentions neither token — so the choice is eliminated by inference rather than by being tried. You can resolve two propositions against each other. You cannot resolve two actions.

The transferable instruction is to treat every requirement for undo as a question about representation before it becomes a question about mechanism. Ask whether the thing you want to be able to take back can be expressed as an assertion made relative to a named assumption, rather than as a change to state, because if it can then withdrawal is just ceasing to believe the name, and all the consequences follow through whatever reasoning the system already performs. The price is not small and should be stated: every derived value in the system must have somewhere to carry its grounds, and that is a commitment made globally rather than at the point where the choice happens. What you get for it is that the code near the choice becomes almost trivial — a device that consults the prohibitions currently in force and believes whichever of its two tokens is not currently ruled out — while the interesting behaviour lives in the substrate that was going to be there anyway.

**Source:** [The Art of the Propagator](../works/the-art-of-the-propagator.md) — the implicit-search subsection's guessing device, which creates two fresh hypothetical premises, writes both alternatives into its place with each supported by one of them, and installs a watcher that keeps any stable belief state committed to exactly one; the accumulation on each premise of sets of other premises that would rule it out, consulted by the device to pick which token to believe; the resolution step performed when neither token can be believed, yielding a prohibition involving neither; and the surrounding machinery in which hypothetical premises are handled by the same worldview and contradiction apparatus as premises describing measurements.
