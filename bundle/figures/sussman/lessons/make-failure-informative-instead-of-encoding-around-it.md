# Put failure in the infrastructure and make it name its causes; a dead end that knows why it failed eliminates a region instead of a point

**Lesson:** A component with several legitimate answers faces a bad choice as long as failure is nobody's job. Return every answer and each consumer must now cope with multiplicity, filter the ones it cannot use, and possibly return multiplicities of its own — a burden that spreads outward through the whole system and couples every consumer to the fact that this producer was ambiguous. Return one answer and hope, which is what conventional practice does, and the producer has silently made a decision it had no information to make, since only the consumer knows what would be acceptable. The escape from the dilemma is a third option that neither party can implement alone: return one answer while retaining the right to be told it was unsuitable and produce another. Deciding whether the ingredients of a computation are acceptable properly belongs to whoever consumes them, and that judgment may itself require computation involving its own tentative choices, so the capability has to be recursive and therefore has to live in the infrastructure rather than in any component.

Giving the infrastructure that job raises the obvious spectre of enumerating everything, and the thing that prevents it is what a failure carries. If reaching a dead end tells you only that the current combination of choices is bad, you have eliminated one point and the work will scale with the size of the space. If the failure instead names which choices were actually responsible — and in problems whose constraints are local, the culprits are typically one or two — then you have eliminated every combination containing that offending subset, which is a substantial slab of the space rather than a point. Record the offending subsets so they are never rediscovered, and let contradictions that block both alternatives of a choice combine into a new prohibition mentioning neither, and the accumulated prohibitions do the pruning. The measured difference on even a small puzzle is dozens of dead ends against hundreds.

The last part is the design instruction, and it cuts against a common instinct. Faced with a search that runs too long, the practised move is to reformulate the problem statement so the wasteful attempts never arise — reorder the choices, hoist the cheap tests earlier, encode the constraints so that violations are unrepresentable. This works, and it defeats the point, because the resulting cleverness is specific to this problem and evaporates the moment the problem changes, while the machinery that extracts information from failure is general and improves every search you will ever run through it. Prefer infrastructure that learns from a mistake over an encoding that avoids the mistake, and notice when you are about to trade a reusable mechanism for a local trick.

**Source:** [The Art of the Propagator](../works/the-art-of-the-propagator.md) — the implicit-search subsection, which opens with the dilemma of a procedure having two valid roots and argues that returning one with the option to change its mind is what makes components independently evolvable, with acceptability being the receiver's responsibility and therefore recursive; the analysis of the occupancy puzzle, noting that because its constraints are local the failure of any configuration is attributable to a unary or binary violation so that studying one failure eliminates large chunks of the space; the guessing machinery, which accumulates reasons against each hypothetical and resolves opposing reasons into a new prohibition involving neither; the reported count of dead ends against the naive figure; and the closing remark that clever reformulations of the problem can reduce the search substantially but defeat much of the purpose of making the search implicit.
