# A thing may be defined in terms of itself whenever consumption provably lags production

**Lesson:** The integers can be defined as a stream beginning with one whose remainder is the elementwise sum of an infinite stream of ones and *the integers themselves*. The Fibonacci numbers are defined as their own elementwise sum with themselves shifted by one place. Neither definition computes anything from the outside; each is a description of the object that refers to the object. They work, and the reason is stated exactly: at any moment, enough of the stream has already been produced that feeding it back into the definition yields the next element. Self-reference is legitimate precisely when every element demanded depends only on elements already generated.

That condition is the transferable content, because it converts self-reference from a stylistic curiosity into something with a check attached. When you want to define a thing in terms of itself, the question is not whether the language permits it but whether there is a lag: does each output depend strictly on earlier outputs? If yes, the definition is well founded and computable one step at a time. If the dependency is on the same position or a later one, the definition is circular in the ruinous sense, and no evaluation strategy rescues it. The stream definitions are safe because addition of position n reaches only positions n minus one.

The book's own most striking case shows how far this reaches. The stream of primes is defined by filtering the integers with a primality test — and that test works by trial division against *the stream of primes*. It terminates because a candidate is settled by primes below its square root, and enough of the stream to cover that always exists by the time the candidate is reached. The authors flag this as very subtle and note that the number-theoretic estimate underwriting it was hard to establish and not proved until the nineteenth century. That is the honest shape of the technique: the definition is trivial to write, and the argument that it terminates can be a real theorem.

The efficiency side carries its own trap and it is the reason the technique needs machinery rather than just permission. A delayed object that recomputes on every access turns the self-referential Fibonacci definition from linear into exponential work, because the same positions are re-derived down every path that reaches them. Memoizing the promise — computing once and storing the result — is what makes the feedback definition cheap, and it is exactly ordinary memoization, with the previously forced part of the stream serving as the table nobody had to write. Laziness alone gives you the expressiveness; laziness with sharing is what gives you the performance, and the two are easy to mistake for one feature.

**Source:** [Structure and Interpretation of Computer Programs](../works/structure-and-interpretation-of-computer-programs.md) - chapter 3 section 3.5.2's subsection on defining streams implicitly, which defines an infinite stream of ones by referring to itself, defines the integers as one consed onto the sum of ones and the integers, defines the Fibonacci stream as itself added to itself shifted one place, and explains that these work because at any point enough of the stream has been generated to feed back into the definition and produce the next element; the alternate definition of the primes by filtering the integers with a primality test that itself iterates over the primes stream, justified by the observation that for every candidate either a generated prime divides it or a generated prime exceeds its square root, with the footnote calling the point very subtle and noting that estimates of this kind were not substantially improved between Euclid and Chebyshev in 1851; together with section 3.5.1's memo-proc implementation of delay and Exercise 3.57, which shows the number of additions for the stream Fibonacci definition would be exponentially greater without that memoization, and its footnote observing that call-by-need effectively constructs the memoization table automatically in the already-forced parts of the stream.
