# A changing quantity and a fixed history are two descriptions of one thing; choosing the second deletes state without deleting the phenomenon

**Lesson:** Having spent a chapter on the costs of assignment, the authors go back and audit the reasoning that led them there. Three decisions were made, each apparently reasonable: model real-world things that have state with computational things that have local variables; identify the passage of time in the world with the passage of time in the computer; implement change in the model as assignment to those variables. Only the first is about the domain. The second and third are choices, and they were adopted without being noticed as choices — which is the general lesson worth taking before any of the technical content. When a line of work leads somewhere unpleasant, the payoff is usually in the assumptions that were never stated, not in the last step.

The alternative is a change of description rather than of mechanism. A quantity that varies over time can be written as a function of time. Look at it instant by instant and it is something that changes; look at the whole history at once and nothing changes at all, because a function is not a thing that varies — it simply *is*, all of it, for every argument. The phenomenon is entirely preserved. Time-dependence is still fully represented. What has vanished is the changing state, and it vanished because of how the same facts were laid out, not because anything was given up.

Discretize time and the function becomes a sequence, possibly infinite, and modelling change becomes computing with sequences that hold the time histories of the things being modelled. That is what makes it more than a philosophical observation: state disappears and the mathematics of sequences, which is well understood and has no ordering hazards, replaces the mathematics of mutation, which is not and does. Practically, this is the same reframing as an event log versus a mutable row, a version history versus a current version, a signal versus a variable — in each case the state-carrying formulation is recoverable from the history, and the history supports questions the state cannot answer.

The authors are careful about the price and so should anyone borrowing the idea. Representing a history as an ordinary list is unusably expensive, so the technique needs delayed evaluation to build the sequence only as far as it is demanded — a piece of machinery, with its own costs, that must be introduced before the approach is practical at all. And they refuse to declare a winner: the stream framework raises difficulties of its own, and which of the two produces more modular and maintainable systems is left open. Both descriptions are legitimate, each buys something specific, and the actual skill is recognizing that you are choosing.

**Source:** [Structure and Interpretation of Computer Programs](../works/structure-and-interpretation-of-computer-programs.md) - the introduction to chapter 3 section 3.5, which reviews the three apparently reasonable decisions that produced the complexity of the chapter (modelling real-world objects with local state as computational objects with local variables, identifying time variation in the world with time variation in the computer, and implementing that variation as assignment), asks whether time in the computer must be identified with time in the modelled world, observes that describing a time-varying quantity as a function of time removes the emphasis on change because the function itself does not change, notes that discrete time lets a time function be modelled as a possibly infinite sequence, states that stream processing lets us model systems that have state without ever using assignment or mutable data, and closes by conceding that the stream framework raises difficulties of its own and that which technique leads to more modular and more easily maintained systems remains open; together with section 3.5.1's demonstration that representing such sequences as ordinary lists is severely inefficient in time and space, motivating delayed evaluation.
