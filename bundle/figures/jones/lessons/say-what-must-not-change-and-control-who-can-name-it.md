# Say explicitly what a component may not touch, and remember the guarantee dies the moment two names can reach one thing

**Lesson:** Any description of what a piece of code does has a silent half — everything it leaves alone — and leaving that half unwritten is a defect rather than an economy, because a reader confronted with an unmentioned quantity cannot tell whether it is promised to stay put or merely not discussed. The fix is cheap: make the set of reachable names part of the interface, mark each one according to whether it may only be read or also changed, and adopt the standing convention that anything absent from the list or marked read-only cannot move. Then the effect description gets to talk only about what actually changes, and the rest is guaranteed by a list short enough to check at a glance.

The marks are load-bearing, not documentation. Knowing that a quantity cannot move collapses a large amount of reasoning that would otherwise have to be carried along: every claim you make about progress through a loop or across a call would need an extra clause saying that this and that are still what they were, and re-establishing those clauses is pure overhead. Restricting write access is therefore a way of buying cheaper reasoning, and it is worth doing even where wider access would have been harmless, which inverts the usual instinct to grant whatever access might conceivably be convenient. It also gives you something to check: code that quietly writes to a name it declared read-only has broken a promise that other people's arguments were resting on.

Now the sting, and it is the part most often left unstated. The whole scheme rests on an assumption about names: that two different ones cannot reach the same thing. The rule that an assignment leaves every claim not mentioning its target still true is simply false when they can, and every frame guarantee built on that rule fails with it. So the discipline holds only where things are passed by value, and any mechanism that reintroduces sharing — a parameter passed by location, a pointer, a heap — has to be paid for rather than assumed away. The way to pay is to make the sharing itself part of what you hold, so that two routes to one object is a fact you can state and reason about instead of an invisible condition that silently voids your reasoning. The general form is worth carrying: a promise about what code leaves alone is a promise about names, and it is exactly as strong as your control over which names can reach the same thing.
