# A method is only usable at scale if no completed design step can be invalidated by a later check

**Lesson:** There is a decisive structural difference between a technique that certifies finished artifacts and a technique you can actually design with, and it is not rigour or expressiveness — it is where the failure lands. A certification technique typically works by building the pieces independently and then running a global compatibility test at the end; when that test fails, everything built on the offending assumption is in question at once, and there is no way to localize the damage. That is tolerable when the pieces are toys and unaffordable when they are months of work. The property you want instead is that each design step, once discharged, is permanently discharged: whatever specifications it hands down are a complete and sufficient description of acceptable implementations, and nothing discovered later can retroactively make the step wrong.

Getting that property is mostly a matter of moving information earlier. If a global check at the end tests some compatibility between components, then whatever that check depends on can instead be written into the components' specifications up front and checked at the moment the decomposition is made — before any of the components exist and before anyone has invested in them. The work is not obviously less; it is simply front-loaded to the point where failure costs one decision rather than an entire subtree of decisions. This is the same economics that makes early type errors preferable to late ones, applied to design commitments rather than to expressions.

The practical test to apply to any method, formal or informal, is therefore: can I hand a subordinate one of these subspecifications and nothing else, and is there any circumstance under which their correct work will later be rejected? If the answer is yes, the method does not decompose and its apparent modularity is an illusion that only shows up as an illusion at integration time. If the answer is no, then the method has genuinely converted one hard problem into several independent smaller ones, and the number of people or the number of months you can throw at it stops being bounded by how much of the whole anyone has to hold in mind.

**Source:** [Tentative Steps Toward a Development Method for Interfering Programs](../works/tentative-steps-toward-a-development-method-for-interfering-programs.md) — the argument opening the extensions section that proving components in isolation and then proving the proofs non-interfering is unacceptable as a development method, and the accompanying claim that never needing a final test whose failure exposes an erroneous assumption is the cardinal property to preserve for large problems. Also [Development Methods for Computer Programs including a Notion of Interference](../works/development-methods-for-computer-programs-including-a-notion-of-interference.md) — the introduction's criterion that a method must supply checks at each stage such that the next stage can be undertaken with confidence, its observation that any aspect of correctness postponed makes all subsequent work a hostage to fortune, and the alternatives chapter's sharper charge that under the prove-then-check-non-interference approach the need for revision is inherent rather than merely a consequence of error.
