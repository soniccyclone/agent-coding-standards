# Expect refinement to resurrect obligations the abstraction had no vocabulary to state

**Lesson:** The appeal of layered refinement is that an obligation discharged at one level stays discharged, so you descend without looking back. That holds only while the lower level makes no distinctions the upper level was incapable of making — and choosing a representation is precisely the act of introducing such distinctions. Replace an unordered collection with a sequence and order exists where it previously could not be spoken of; replace one shared quantity with several whose combination stands for it and the individual parts have behaviours the whole never had. An implementation is free to depend on the new distinctions, and the moment it does, the argument that the pieces fit together must be made again at the concrete level, because the abstract version of that argument was silent on the property now being relied upon.

This is not a defect in the method and the response is not to forbid it. The response is to know which obligations are of this kind — the ones about how components interact, as opposed to the ones about what each component achieves — and to expect to re-establish exactly those after each representation choice. The cost is a repeated proof, not a discarded design. What would be a genuine defect is not noticing: silently inheriting a compatibility argument that was only ever valid in a vocabulary where the relevant property could not be uttered.

Two consequences follow for how you sequence design decisions. First, strictly top-down discipline has to be relaxed in one specific way: when a lower level's viability depends on a property that will only become expressible later, that property must be anticipated and written into the specification now, or the descent leads somewhere that cannot be implemented. Documenting a design as top-down does not license thinking top-down only. Second, some steps genuinely cannot be split. If the conditions governing how parts interfere have no meaning until a representation exists, then choosing the representation and splitting the work into parts are one decision, and forcing them into two steps produces either an abstract step that over-commits the parts or a concrete step that merely transcribes the abstract one and decides nothing. Refusing to subdivide a step is sometimes the honest move.
