# Engineer progress to arrive in quanta bigger than your measurement error, and give the comparator a neutral band

**Lesson:** A process that decides each step by measuring whether a candidate is better than the incumbent has two failure modes that pull in opposite directions. Measure with a strict comparison and you chase noise: sampling error alone will make some worse candidate look better, and the process wanders. Demand a large improvement and you stall: real progress that happens to be small gets rejected, and the process freezes short of the goal. The way out is not to tune between them but to restructure the analysis so the two problems are decoupled. Prove first that every available step either improves things by at least some definite amount or does not improve them at all — no continuum in between. Progress now arrives in quanta. Then set the decision threshold below the quantum and the measurement precision fine enough to resolve the threshold, and the classification of every candidate becomes reliable rather than probabilistic in any way that matters.

Notice the order of design, because it is the reverse of the intuitive one. The quantum comes from the structure of the state space and the moves, and it is discovered by case analysis, not chosen. The threshold is then a consequence of the quantum. The number of observations per candidate is a consequence of the threshold, via whatever concentration bound applies. And the total number of observations is a consequence of that times the number of comparisons the whole run will make, which finally has to be small enough that a union bound over every comparison still leaves the failure probability under budget. Each quantity is forced by the previous one; nothing is a free parameter. When someone asks how large a sample you need, the honest answer requires walking that chain backwards, and if the chain has no quantum at its far end, then no sample size is defensible.

The threshold buys a second thing that is easy to overlook: a third verdict. A candidate can now be better, worse, or *within tolerance* — and the middle category is load-bearing rather than a rounding artifact. A process that only ever accepts strict improvements has nowhere to go once no improving move exists, even when the goal is still far away; permitting moves that provably cost no more than the tolerance lets it keep travelling, and because the tolerance is bounded above, that travel cannot accumulate into real loss. Cleanup phases especially need this. Dismantling accumulated cruft is often individually neutral rather than individually beneficial, so a strict comparator forbids exactly the moves that tidy up, and the process is left permanently carrying the scaffolding it used to get where it is.
