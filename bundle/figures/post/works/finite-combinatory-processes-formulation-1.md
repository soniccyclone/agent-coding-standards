A "problem solver" (or machine) acts on a two-way-infinite sequence of boxes using a fixed, tiny vocabulary of primitive acts — mark a box, erase a box, move to the adjacent box, and test-and-branch on whether the current box is marked — carried out by following a numbered list of directions. The three-page note is explicit that this is offered as an analysis of the outer bound of what any human clerk following fixed mechanical rules could ever do, giving the Church-Turing thesis a third, independent angle of attack.
