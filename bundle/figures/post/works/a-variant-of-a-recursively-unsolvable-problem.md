description: Introduces what's now called the Post correspondence problem — given two ordered lists of strings over a shared alphabet, decide whether some sequence of (repeatable) indices makes the two lists' concatenations match. Post proves the problem recursively unsolvable by reducing it to unsolvability results he'd already established for Thue-style rewriting systems and the halting problem. Its clean, easy-to-state combinatorial form made it one of the most frequently reused reduction targets for later undecidability proofs, well outside logic proper.
