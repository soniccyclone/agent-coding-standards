# Trade generality for tractability on purpose, and keep a ledger of what the trade cost you

**Lesson:** Generality is usually adopted by default, on the reasoning that someone might need it and it costs nothing to allow. It costs a great deal. Permit activities to be created and destroyed at arbitrary times and the language's meaning and its implementation both get harder; permit self-reference and you can no longer compute how much storage anything needs; permit an unbounded variety of workloads and you forfeit every optimization that depends on knowing what usually happens. The deliberate move is to look at what your generality is actually used for, discover that in practice it is used to build one fixed arrangement, and then make the fixed arrangement the rule. Static resource requirements, no fragmentation, and calls between protected modules running at nearly the cost of ordinary calls all fall out of decisions to forbid things — and the same reasoning applies one level up, in the observation that specialized systems succeed while general-purpose ones disappoint precisely because restricting the class of work admitted is what lets a designer exploit what that work is like.

The same instinct should govern notation, and here it cuts against the usual aesthetic. Given a choice between a terse, symmetric formulation and a redundant, asymmetric one, prefer the redundant one when the redundancy lets a translator determine statically what it would otherwise have to discover at run time — how many callers a component can have, how much storage it needs, whether an interaction is well-typed. Brevity is a property of the text; the ability to size and check the program before it runs is a property of the whole engineering situation.

The part that makes this a real lesson rather than an excuse is the ledger. Restriction is not free even when it is right, and its costs arrive late and disguised. The choices that made a first implementation tractable were the same ones that hid recursive process structures from their author for fifteen years, because a concept you have forbidden yourself is a concept you stop being able to see. So the discipline has two halves: cut generality hard, on evidence of actual use rather than on imagined need, and write down what you cut and why, because you will need that list when the restriction later looks like a law of nature. There is also a real and underrated payoff in morale — a problem that seemed impossible becomes manageable the moment enough of it is declared out of scope.
