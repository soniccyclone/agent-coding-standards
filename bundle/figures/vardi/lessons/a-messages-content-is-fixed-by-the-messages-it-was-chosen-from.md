# A message's content is fixed by the messages it was chosen from, not by how much it narrows anyone's uncertainty

**Lesson:** The convenient model of a message is a quantity of information handed over, and two variants of Vardi's puzzle demolish it. Take the vague announcement — that at least one of the participants is in a particular condition — and replace it with something strictly more specific, naming which one. Every recipient's uncertainty about the world is now smaller, and the group's ability to reach the conclusion it would otherwise have reached is gone. The collective deduction was never running on how much anyone knew; it was running on the participants being interchangeable with respect to the announcement, because each one could only advance the chain by simulating the others, and the simulation was licensed by the symmetry. Naming an individual destroys the symmetry, and every inference that depended on it dies with it.

The positive form of the same law is sharper still. Leave the sender's words untouched and change only the set of things the sender might have said instead — establish among the participants that the sender either makes the informative announcement or else says something irrelevant — and a deduction that fails without that establishment now succeeds, even when one participant may not have been paying attention. So the content of a message is not a property of the message. It is a property of the choice the message came out of: which alternatives were open to the sender, and which of those the receivers can rely on having been open. The same bytes on the same wire carry different content under two protocols that differ only in what else the sender was permitted to emit.

Three consequences are worth carrying into ordinary design. Absence is a premise: the argument works partly because the participants can count on nothing further having been said, which means that adding a well-intentioned extra notification can invalidate a previously sound protocol argument no matter how carefully the new notification is worded, since the damage is done by the widened alternative set rather than by the words. Symmetry can be load-bearing: before enriching a broadcast with recipient-specific detail, ask whether the recipients' interchangeability under it is what makes the coordination work, because a uniform announcement and a set of individually addressed ones are different mechanisms even with identical payloads. And the admissible message space at each point of a protocol belongs in the specification rather than being left to implementers, since quietly widening it changes the meaning of every message already in it — including messages whose format nobody touched.

**Source:** [Reasoning About Knowledge](../works/reasoning-about-knowledge.md) — chapter two's exercise replacing the existential announcement with one naming a specific child, which asks the reader to explain why the group can no longer solve the puzzle despite apparently having more information, and notes that the puzzle also depends on the assumption that nothing further was said; and the preceding exercise in which it is established among the children that the father says either the informative sentence or a vacuous one, so that a child who may not have been listening still knows which alternatives were available, and the deduction survives.
