description: Bachman's 1973 Turing Award lecture, arguing that the shift from sequential, file-based processing to large shared databases with complex pointer-linked structures changes the programmer's basic task from batch calculation to real-time "navigation" through a network of records. He contends the database management system is absorbing responsibilities that used to belong to the operating system, and sketches what that means for how programmers should think about data access. It's Bachman's own framing of why the navigational/network model he built at GE mattered, delivered at the height of its influence and just before Codd's relational model began to displace it.
