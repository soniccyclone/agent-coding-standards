# Calibrate your standard for a good abstraction on a field that already has good ones

**Lesson:** When you are building the framework for a young area, the hardest missing thing is not technique but a standard — some way to tell whether what you have written down is a good theory or merely a consistent one. A young area cannot supply that standard, because its own current practice is what you are trying to improve on. Older disciplines can, and the marks of quality they exhibit turn out not to be about their subject matter at all: one measured constraint, taken seriously, forcing the abandonment of something everyone had assumed was self-evident; conclusions previously reachable only through heavy machinery re-derived with elementary means; a small set of stated commitments from which the surprising consequences follow rather than being appended. Someone who has watched a mature theory do that carries a usable template into a field that has none, and recognizes a weak framework long before it fails.

Two of those marks deserve to be applied directly to design work. The first is that a good abstraction earns its place by killing an assumption, not by organizing what you already believed; if adopting it leaves every prior belief intact, it is notation. The second is that the ability to reach advanced conclusions with elementary machinery is evidence about the abstraction, not about the audience — when a framework demands heavy apparatus for results that ought to be a couple of steps from its definitions, the definitions are carrying the wrong content. Both marks are checkable on your own work in an afternoon, which is what makes an imported standard more useful than a local convention.

The corollary is a pragmatic one about method purity. Having a template for what a good theory looks like is compatible with, and in fact demands, complete indifference about where the machinery comes from: the obligation is that the problem be genuinely solved, so a result borrowed from an adjacent discipline, an existing formalism bent to a new use, and a purpose-built argument are all equally admissible, chosen on fit rather than provenance. This is also why arriving in a field from outside is an asset rather than a handicap. The outsider is the person most likely to be holding a standard the field has not yet developed, and least likely to mistake the field's habits for necessities.

**Source:** [A Journey Through Computer Science](../works/a-journey-through-computer-science.md) — the concluding remarks, where the two lasting benefits of a prior training in physics are named as knowing what good theories look like and a pragmatic willingness to use, learn, or invent whatever method solves the problem at hand, together with the early-enchantment section's two exemplars: a derivation in which the measured constancy of light's speed dislodges absolute time, and a lecture series showing that advanced concepts admit explanation by elementary mathematics.
