# Demote your special machinery to a technique and let the standard setting carry the story

**Lesson:** Work that grows out of a particular apparatus tends to be presented in that apparatus, and the presentation then inherits an ad hoc quality that has nothing to do with the results. The reader meets an unfamiliar structure defined by conditions chosen to make later proofs work, and has no independent reason to believe the structure is natural — so the whole development reads as a construction built to order. The remedy is to look for a formulation whose primitives are already standard in some established field, state the problem and the results there, and reintroduce your machinery as an analytical technique used inside the proofs. Scott reports doing exactly this after a conference and two lecture series: he found he could describe the work in purely topological terms, leaving the lattices as a special technique of analysis, and says the effect was to make the presentation much less ad hoc and to connect the results to standard point-set topology in a far more understandable way.

Note what this is not. It is not simplification, and it is not a change of content — the same theorems get proved by the same arguments. It is a decision about which vocabulary is load-bearing at the top level. When the standard vocabulary is on the outside, your results become claims that a reader of the established field can evaluate, compare with their existing knowledge, and connect to neighboring literature, and your special structure earns its keep by being demonstrably useful for proving those claims rather than by being assumed at the start. The specialized apparatus also becomes easier to justify, because it now appears at the point of need with a visible job to do.

There is a cost to know about: the reordering is real work, it happens after the mathematics is finished, and it does not generalize from the discovery order. The order in which ideas arrived is almost never the order that makes them credible, and the gap between the two is not laziness but a separate design problem. The signal that you have this problem is the sense that your own definitions look arbitrary when you read them cold — which is worth taking seriously rather than attributing to the reader's unfamiliarity.

**Source:** [Continuous Lattices](../works/continuous-lattices.md) — the acknowledgements at the head of the references, which record that the presentation changed considerably after the January conference and subsequent lectures, that the outcome was a purely topological description with the lattices demoted to a special technique of analysis, and that this made the presentation much less ad hoc and related the results to standard point-set topology far more understandably. The introduction's framing of the paper as an argument that a neglected class of topological spaces is natural rather than merely useful is the same choice carried out.
