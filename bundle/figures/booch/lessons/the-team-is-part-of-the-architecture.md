# The organization is part of the structure, so restructuring the system means restructuring the people

**Lesson:** A system's decomposition and the arrangement of the people who build it converge on each other, and the convergence runs both directions. The structure of the group leaves its imprint on the artifact, and the artifact then entrenches the group that produced it, because each part now has an owner with a budget and a reputation attached. The practical consequence is uncomfortable: an intended change to the decomposition that leaves the group arrangement intact will be resisted by the group arrangement and will lose. Which means the design work and the organizational work are the same work, and the organizational half is the harder half, because it is conducted in a medium of careers rather than code.

This explains a failure mode that looks inexplicable from a purely technical vantage. Well-funded groups of capable people, in possession of a correct technical understanding of their problem, fail on it, and the reason is invariably that the human arrangement could not deliver the technical arrangement. It also explains why fragmenting development along cost lines destroys the capability being optimized: the coordination that innovation actually consists of is the thing being cut, and the parts that remain are the parts that were already routine. Distribution across sites and time zones has the same character in weaker form; it does not prevent good work, but it removes the incidental contact in which shared understanding is normally formed, so that contact has to be manufactured deliberately if it is to exist at all.

Two operational consequences follow. First, the structure of the group should be designed rather than inherited, including the unglamorous parts: who represents whose interests when a cross-cutting decision is made, and where the people responsible for coherence sit relative to the people delivering. A coherence function staffed by people detached from any running project produces artifacts nobody can use and loses its authority within a release or two. Second, expect that any serious structural change in the artifact will require dismantling something in the organization, and price that in from the start rather than discovering it midway. A programmer who accepts this reads a proposed decomposition as a proposed reporting structure, and treats a design that no plausible team arrangement could deliver as an unbuildable design.

**Source:** [Architecting the Unknown](../works/architecting-the-unknown.md) — the argument that architects must also work as social scientists, that a system's structure and its team's structure reflect one another, and that restructuring one therefore requires restructuring the other against egos and budgets. Also [The Future of Software Engineering](../works/the-future-of-software-engineering.md) on the progression from small colocated groups to teams of teams and on the loss of capability when development is fragmented purely for cost; [The Promise, the Limits, and the Beauty of Software](../works/the-promise-the-limits-and-the-beauty-of-software.md) on organizing coherence work as representation from the delivering groups rather than as a detached function; and [Building the Enchanted Land](../works/building-the-enchanted-land.md), which lists team structure alongside software structure among the limits on turning intent into a system.
