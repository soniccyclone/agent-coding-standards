# Check the supply of independent primitives before you design around them

**Lesson:** A construction that improves a weak test by combining many independent copies of it carries a hidden requirement that nobody writes down: that many independent copies exist. Where the primitives are generated by a parameter with an enormous range, the supply is effectively unlimited and the requirement never surfaces. Where the primitives are indexed by something small — the coordinates of a vector, the fields of a record, the sensors on a device, the distinct labels in a schema — the supply is a hard, countable resource, and it caps how far the composition can be pushed no matter how much compute is available. The steepness you can buy is bounded by the inventory, not by the budget.

This deserves to be checked at the point where the primitive family is chosen, because it is the kind of constraint that shows up late and cannot be engineered around. Two families can look interchangeable, offer the same relationship between closeness and agreement, and have the same cost per evaluation, while one of them admits unlimited amplification and the other runs out after a few dozen members. Discovering this after building the pipeline leaves you with a decision function whose transition is permanently too shallow, and the usual reflex — reuse the primitives, drawing the same ones more than once — silently violates the independence that the whole analysis assumed. Reused draws do not compound; they agree with each other, and the composite is no better than its parts while appearing to be.

The other half of the check is the scale of the primitive's output. A family borrowed from a different setting may report on a range that does not match what the composition operators expect, and the fix is trivial arithmetic. It is worth separating these two kinds of mismatch when auditing a substitution: one is a rescaling that costs nothing, the other is a shortage that constrains the design. Treating them with the same seriousness wastes attention on the harmless one and can leave the fatal one unexamined, since both present initially as "this family is not quite like the one the theory was written for."

The transferable habit is to ask, of any scheme that says "use many independent instances," how many instances there are in total, and whether that count is fixed by the structure of the domain or grows with something you control. If it is fixed, put it in the design notes next to the memory budget, because it is that kind of number. If it grows, note what it grows with, because that is the knob you will reach for when the error rates turn out to be unacceptable.

**Source:** [Mining of Massive Datasets](../works/mining-of-massive-datasets.md) — chapter 3's construction of a locality-sensitive family for Hamming distance, whose functions read a single coordinate of the vector, together with the observation that this family differs from the minhash family in exactly two ways: the distances need rescaling by the dimension, which is of no consequence, and the family contains only as many functions as there are coordinates, which limits how many can be composed and therefore how steep the resulting curve can be made.
