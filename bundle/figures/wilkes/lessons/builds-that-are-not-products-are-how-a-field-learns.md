# Keep building things that are not meant to become products, especially when the unit of composition changes

**Lesson:** The knowledge of what works, and more valuably of what is not worth doing, does not come from artifacts that had to succeed. It accumulates from builds undertaken with no intention that they become prototypes for anything, because only those are free to explore a decision to its actual conclusion rather than stopping when the schedule or the market says stop. A field that has only product-grade construction has plenty of working systems and no reliable body of negative results, which is the half of the knowledge that prevents repeated expensive mistakes. Notably, the surrounding commercial output can look like it never absorbed the lessons and the diversity of exploratory building can still have paid for itself many times over, because the payoff is in the shared understanding rather than in any particular built thing.

There are two freedoms worth naming here, and they are not the same. Freedom from commercial pressure buys the ability to work on something that will not pay. Freedom from the need to follow fashion buys the ability to work on something nobody currently thinks is interesting, and it is the rarer and more valuable of the two — institutions can be commercially insulated and still entirely fashion-driven, at which point they have bought the wrong freedom.

The moment this matters most is when the grain of construction shifts. When the parts you compose stop being small homogeneous components and become large subsystems with their own behaviour, the accumulated competence at the old grain does not carry over: composing subsystems into a working whole is a different discipline from composing components into a working part, and it has to be learned the same way the first one was — by building things at the new grain that are allowed to fail. Any field going through such a shift should expect to need a new round of unfunded-by-results exploration precisely when its previous success makes that look unnecessary.

**Source:** [Computers Then and Now](../works/computers-then-and-now.md) — the pioneering-days argument that groups building experimental computers not intended for serial production created the body of knowledge about what was and was not profitable to do, the call for the same diversity now that the task is composing memory modules, processors and peripherals into systems, and the account of what universities contribute through freedom from commercial considerations and from following fashion.
