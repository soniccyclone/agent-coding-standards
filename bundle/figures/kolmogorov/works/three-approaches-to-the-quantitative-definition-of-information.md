description: Surveys combinatorial and probabilistic ways of measuring "how much information" a message carries, then introduces a third: define the information content of an individual object as the length of the shortest program that produces it on a fixed universal computer. This algorithmic approach sidesteps the need for a probability distribution over an ensemble of possible messages, letting complexity be defined for a single object in isolation. It's the paper that launched what's now called Kolmogorov complexity, arrived at independently of (and shortly after) Solomonoff's and around the same time as Chaitin's versions of the same idea.
