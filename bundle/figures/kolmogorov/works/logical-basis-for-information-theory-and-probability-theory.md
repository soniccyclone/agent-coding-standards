description: A short program-setting paper arguing that both probability theory and information theory can be re-grounded in the theory of algorithms rather than in classical measure-theoretic probability. Instead of calling a single object "random" because it was drawn from some ensemble with a distribution, Kolmogorov proposes calling it random when it is incompressible — when no short program can generate it. This reframing of randomness as a property of individual objects, rather than of populations, seeded the later theory of algorithmic randomness (Martin-Loef and others).
