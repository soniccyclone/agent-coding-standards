description: Kolmogorov's short monograph puts probability theory on a rigorous axiomatic footing by identifying it with measure theory - a probability space is a set of outcomes, a sigma-algebra of measurable events over it, and a countably additive measure assigning each event a value between 0 and 1. Before this, probability was argued about informally in terms of frequencies and symmetry, with no agreed formal foundation comparable to what Hilbert-style axiomatization had already given geometry and arithmetic. Included here as a boundary case per the figure's own why-candidate note: it's a foundational act of formalization by the same hand that later formalized algorithmic complexity, but its content is classical mathematics, not computation.
