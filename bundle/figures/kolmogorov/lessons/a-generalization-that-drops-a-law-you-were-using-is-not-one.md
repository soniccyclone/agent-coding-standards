# Judge a proposed generalization by which laws it keeps, not by how much more it covers

**Lesson:** Kolmogorov's definition of expectation only applies when a certain absolute-value integral is finite, which leaves out distributions with heavy symmetric tails. There is an obvious way to widen it: take the symmetric truncated integral and let the cutoff grow. He raises this himself and then rejects it in the form proposed, because the widened notion loses additivity — the value assigned to a sum is no longer the sum of the values. His verdict is that in this form the generalization is barely admissible at all. Not wrong, not paradoxical, just not worth having: the reason anyone reached for expectation was that it obeys laws, and a definition that covers more objects while obeying fewer laws has traded away the thing being extended. He then shows how to earn it back. Instead of justifying the wider definition by the formula that computes it, he characterizes it operationally — is there a constant that the running means of independent copies converge to? — and proves that such a constant exists exactly when the truncated limit exists and the tail decays fast enough. Under that side condition the extension becomes natural, agrees with the old definition wherever the old one applies, still satisfies the properties he actually uses downstream, and is genuinely wider, which he establishes by exhibiting a density where the new value exists and the old integral diverges. He also states what still does not survive: the extension of the absolute value can fail to exist even when the extension of the quantity does.

The general discipline is that a generalization has to be evaluated against the laws its predecessor satisfied, one by one, and every casualty has to be either accepted out loud or bought back with a hypothesis. The instinct to reach for the formula is what goes wrong. A formula that produces a value on more inputs looks like progress and is the wrong artifact to be reasoning about, because the value was never the point — the algebra was. The productive alternative is the one Kolmogorov takes: find the operational property that made the original object useful, promote *that* to the definition, and let the class of objects it applies to be whatever it turns out to be. A definition derived this way comes with its own scope, agreement with the special case is a theorem rather than a hope, and the side conditions announce themselves instead of being discovered later by whoever relied on a law that quietly stopped holding.

In software this is the recurring failure of the widened interface. A comparison relaxed to handle mixed types and no longer transitive, so every sort built on it is undefined. A merge relaxed to accept conflicting updates and no longer associative or commutative, so replicas that received the same writes in different orders diverge. A retry wrapper applied to operations that are not idempotent. A cache keyed on a coarser identity than the function's real arguments. In each case the extension is defended by pointing at inputs it now accepts, and in each case what broke was an algebraic law that everything downstream had silently assumed. So the review question for any proposed widening is not what it now supports but which of the old equations still hold on the new domain — and if one is lost, whether the callers depending on it can be enumerated. If they cannot, the honest move is a separate named operation with its own weaker contract rather than a quiet relaxation of the existing one.

**Source:** [Grundbegriffe der Wahrscheinlichkeitsrechnung](../works/grundbegriffe-der-wahrscheinlichkeitsrechnung.md) — Chapter VI, §4, which proposes the symmetric-limit generalization of mathematical expectation, notes that additivity over sums fails and calls the generalization in that form hardly admissible, then recharacterizes it by the existence of a constant to which the arithmetic means of independent identically distributed copies converge, gives the necessary and sufficient conditions for that, records which of the original properties still hold, remarks that the generalized expectation of the absolute value need not exist, and supplies a density witnessing that the new notion is strictly wider than the old.
