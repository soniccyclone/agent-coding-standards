# When no formula is available, define the thing by the constraint every observation of it must satisfy

**Lesson:** Conditioning on a partition with infinitely many parts has no formula behind it — the ratio that defines conditional probability in the finite case divides by zero. Kolmogorov's response is to stop trying to compute the object and instead write down the one law it must obey: averaging the conditional value over any region of conditioning outcomes must reproduce the unconditional value restricted to that region, for every such region of positive probability. That is the entire definition. Nothing tells you what the object is at any individual point; the specification is a family of constraints indexed by all possible observations of it. Two obligations then have to be discharged before the definition means anything, and he discharges both explicitly. Uniqueness: a quantity is pinned down by its averages over all regions, up to disagreement on a set of probability zero, which he gets from a property of the integral proved earlier for exactly this purpose. Existence: the constraint family has a solution, which he obtains by verifying the hypotheses of a general representation theorem rather than by exhibiting a construction. Only afterwards does he give a constructive route, as a limit of sums built from conditional probabilities — and he validates it not by comparing it to the abstract object but by checking that it satisfies the same defining constraint, which is what makes the two definitions the same definition.

The technique generalizes to anything whose direct construction is blocked. Rather than defining an object by how to compute it, define it by what every measurement of it must agree with; then prove that the constraints determine it up to whatever equivalence you can actually tolerate, and separately that something satisfies them. This is more work up front and it buys three things that a construction does not. The specification is stated in terms of quantities the caller already understands, so it can be checked without understanding the implementation. Alternative implementations become interchangeable by construction — proving a new one correct means proving it satisfies the constraints, never comparing it to another implementation. And the two failure modes are separated: a specification with no solution is over-constrained, one with many solutions is under-constrained, and each has a different repair. Conflating them is why "it's ambiguous" and "it's impossible" get diagnosed as the same bug.

The engineering counterpart is the difference between specifying an operation by its algorithm and specifying it by the invariants relating it to everything else in the system. A scheduler defined as "the queue is drained in this order" versus one defined by the properties any admissible schedule must have. A cache defined by its eviction code versus one defined by the requirement that a read observe some value the underlying store held at or after the read's start. A reconciler defined as a loop versus one defined by the fixed point it must reach. The constraint form is what lets you swap the implementation, test against a reference oracle, or run two implementations in parallel and compare — because there is a statement of correctness that does not mention either of them. And when nothing satisfies your constraints, you have learned something genuinely useful early: the operation you were about to build cannot exist in the form you imagined, which is far better news before the implementation than after it.

**Source:** [Grundbegriffe der Wahrscheinlichkeitsrechnung](../works/grundbegriffe-der-wahrscheinlichkeitsrechnung.md) — Chapter V, §1, where conditional probability with respect to a function is defined by the requirement that its average over every set of conditioning values with positive probability agree with the corresponding unconditional probability, uniqueness up to equivalence is derived from the earlier integral property that a variable is determined by its integrals over all sets, and existence is obtained by checking the hypotheses of Nikodym's theorem; together with Chapter V, §4, which builds conditional expectation constructively as a limit of sums and then proves it correct by verifying it satisfies the same defining relation.
