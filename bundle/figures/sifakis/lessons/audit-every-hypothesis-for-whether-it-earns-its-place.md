# Audit each assumption twice: drop what the argument never uses, adopt what is free in practice and collapses cases

**Lesson:** Assumptions accumulate in a design by inheritance. You are working inside a setting where the objects have rich structure, so every lemma gets stated about objects with that structure, whether or not the reasoning touches it. Sifakis and co-authors visibly resist this. Their central transfer lemma is stated for arbitrary order-preserving maps, and they attach a note explaining that the maps in every one of their own applications come with far more structure than that, that they checked whether the extra structure would let them weaken the lemma's other requirements, and that it would not — so the lemma stays at the weaker hypothesis. Nothing about the paper's use of it changes. What changes is that the lemma now applies to situations the authors did not have in mind, and that a reader can see precisely which property of the map is load-bearing.

The audit runs the other way too, and the same paper does that as well. Their notion of correspondence between models comes in a plain form and a slightly strengthened form that also constrains behavior backwards. The plain form leaves them with four different ways to represent a system as a function on properties, each preserving a different and incomplete slice of the specification language — one slice for future claims, another for past claims, another for existential ones. Rather than live with the taxonomy, they observe that the strengthened correspondence is barely a restriction on real systems and that adopting it collapses several slices into one: both future and past modalities are preserved at once. The same judgment call appears again in the composition theorems, where side conditions are kept even though weaker ones would do, on the stated grounds that the stronger ones hold almost always in practice and buy a cleaner statement.

So the discipline is not "minimize assumptions." It is to price each assumption separately against what it delivers, and to be willing to move in either direction. An assumption the argument never consults is pure loss: it narrows applicability and misleads the reader about what matters. An assumption that costs nothing on the artifacts you will actually encounter, and that eliminates a case split or a family of near-duplicate results, is a bargain even though it is formally a restriction. The failure mode on both sides is the same — never having examined the assumption at all, and inheriting it from context. Go through the hypotheses of anything you have proved, or the preconditions of anything you have written, and for each one ask what breaks if it goes and what merges if it stays.

**Source:** [Property Preserving Abstractions for the Verification of Concurrent Systems](../works/property-preserving-abstractions-1995.md) — the remark preceding section 5's preservation theorem that only monotonicity of the two maps is used and that the availability of Galois adjoints does not permit weakening its conditions; section 6.3's closing argument that representing systems by the forward image function preserves only past modalities and that adopting forward-and-backward simulation, not really a constraint in practice, yields both; and section 7.2's note that slightly weaker side conditions would suffice but the stronger ones are almost always satisfied.
