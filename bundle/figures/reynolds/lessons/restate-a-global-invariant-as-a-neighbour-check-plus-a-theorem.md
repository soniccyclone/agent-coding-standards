# Restate a global invariant as a neighbour check plus a theorem, or you cannot repair anything incrementally

**Lesson:** The natural way to say what a structure must satisfy is usually a statement about every pair of elements standing in some relation, however far apart they sit. That formulation is right, and it is useless for maintaining the structure. You cannot check it cheaply, you cannot see which local edit endangers it, and after any modification the only honest thing the statement supports is re-verifying everything. So the working move is to invent a second formulation phrased entirely in terms of an element and its immediate neighbours — is this one consistent with the one above it, is it consistent with the ones below it — and then discharge a theorem connecting the two: if the local conditions hold everywhere, the global statement follows.

The theorem is where the effort goes, and it is worth being explicit that this is a real proof obligation and not a restatement. Locality does not automatically imply globality; it does so here because the relation is generated by the neighbour steps, so any distant pair is connected by a chain of local ones and the property composes along the chain. That is a fact about the particular structure, and the same move fails on structures where distant constraints are not chains of near ones. Doing the proof is also what tells you the exact local conditions you need — usually a small family of them, one per direction, each defined to be trivially true when the neighbour is absent, so that boundary elements need no separate treatment.

What you buy is a program that can touch the structure. Every operation now reduces to establishing a handful of neighbour conditions at a handful of sites, so its cost is proportional to the depth of the chains rather than the size of the structure, and its correctness argument is a citation of the theorem rather than a new global argument. The wider habit is worth naming, because the same trap recurs far from data structures: a system property stated over all components at once — every replica agrees, every cached value matches its source, every reference resolves — is a specification, not a maintenance plan. Find the neighbour-level condition whose universal truth implies it, prove the implication once, and then let every operation work locally.

**Source:** [The Craft of Programming](../works/the-craft-of-programming.md) — Section 5.2.3, which states the heap property as an invariant relating arbitrarily distant positions in the tree — weight increases along reachability — then observes that an alternative is to focus on the relationship between a member and its immediate neighbours, defines predicates asserting that a position is consistent with its father and with each of its sons (each vacuously true when the neighbour is absent), lists the elementary properties connecting these to the global heap condition, and proves the non-trivial theorem that if the structure is a tree and the heap condition holds everywhere except one position, and that position is consistent both upward and downward, then the global heap condition holds — with a proof that works by routing any distant pair through the father or a son of the exceptional position.
