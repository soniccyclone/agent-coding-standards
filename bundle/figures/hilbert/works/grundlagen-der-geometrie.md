description: Hilbert's 1899 axiomatization of Euclidean geometry, replacing the diagram-and-intuition style of proof in Euclid's Elements with an explicit, complete set of axioms about undefined primitive objects and relations. It is the clearest working example of Hilbert's method in practice, strip a domain down to a minimal formal rule-set and derive everything else from it, with the meaning of "point" or "line" treated as irrelevant to validity. That same move, that only the rules of derivation matter and not the intended interpretation of the symbols, is what the later formalist program tried to generalize to all of mathematics.
