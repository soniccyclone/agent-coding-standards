# A restriction plus an escape hatch that undoes it is worse than either extreme: you keep the whole cost of the discipline and lose the property it was for

Russell and Whitehead accepted an argument that certain definitions are illegitimate — those that define a thing by reference to a totality the thing itself belongs to — and built the restriction into their system by stratifying variables into levels, with substitution allowed only from strictly lower levels. That stratification is not cheap. It multiplies the variables, it splits every principle into an infinite family of level-indexed versions, and it makes the whole apparatus harder to state and harder to work in. Then it turned out that classical mathematics could not be developed inside the restricted system the way they wanted. Their response was to add an axiom asserting that for any object at any level there exists an equivalent one at the bottom level.

Church's verdict is unusually blunt for him. The effect of that axiom, he notes, is largely to restore the very possibility the levels were introduced to eliminate. The true choice is between the unstratified system and the stratified one without the extra axiom; he says it is hard to think of a point of view from which the middle position would look significant. And he records that Russell himself, in the second edition, recommended abandoning it.

The structure is exact and it recurs everywhere. A discipline is imposed to guarantee some property. The discipline turns out to block something needed. Rather than choosing — accept the loss, or conclude the discipline was wrong — an exemption is added that recovers the blocked capability. The result is strictly dominated by both endpoints. The full cost of the discipline is still paid, in ceremony, in machinery, in the effort every participant spends satisfying it. The guarantee is gone, because the exemption reintroduces exactly what was excluded. Nobody notices, because the apparatus is all still visibly there, and the presence of apparatus is what people read as the presence of a guarantee.

The engineering instances are easy to enumerate once the shape is clear. A static type system with a universal escape cast, used at every boundary where the types get hard. A sandbox with a privileged interface reachable from inside it. An immutability discipline with a mutation backdoor. A linter with blanket suppression comments, a permissions model whose admin role is what everything actually runs as, a layering rule with an exception list, a code freeze with an expedited path. In every case the discipline still costs what it costs, and the property it was supposed to buy is not held. The tell is always the same: the exemption is used on precisely the cases the discipline was aimed at, because those are the cases that were hard, which is why the exemption was created.

The decision this argues for is the uncomfortable one. When a constraint blocks something you need, treat that as information about the constraint. Either the need is illegitimate and should be given up, or the constraint is wrong and should be dropped, or — the only good middle — the exemption is narrow, its scope is stated in advance, and there is a real argument that the property survives inside the reduced scope. What must not happen is the exemption that is granted case by case wherever the constraint bites, since that converges on the unconstrained system while retaining the paperwork. And there is a diagnostic worth applying to any existing discipline: look at where the escape hatch actually gets used. If it is used in the region the discipline was designed to police, you are in the intermediate position, and you should pick an end.

**Source:** [Introduction to Mathematical Logic](../works/introduction-to-mathematical-logic.md) — the section on the axioms of reducibility, which describes their adoption in Principia Mathematica because classical mathematics could not otherwise be developed in the ramified system, records the criticism that their effect is largely to restore the impredicative definitions the level distinction was designed to eliminate, states Church's judgment that the real choice lies between the simple calculi and the ramified calculi without these axioms with no evident point of view favouring the intermediate position, and notes Russell's own later recommendation to abandon them.
