# Reduction bottoms out at the act of combining, and a regress that reappears one level up is a floor rather than a step

Church separates a language's symbols into those that mean something on their own and those that mean nothing on their own but make longer meaningful expressions when combined with the first kind. Brackets are the obvious case of the second kind; so is every notation for joining operands into a compound. Then he asks the reduction question a minimalist would ask: can the second kind be eliminated? Mostly yes. Any joining notation can be traded for the name of the function it corresponds to, extending the vocabulary if necessary, and the compound is then written as that function applied to its operands. What he shows next is that this program cannot run to completion. The notation for applying a function to its argument is itself one of these meaningless-in-isolation combining devices. Trade it for a name, and you now need the notation for applying a two-argument function; trade that, and you need three; and so on with nothing gained at any step. The regress is not a hard case awaiting a cleverer trick. It is the shape of a genuine floor.

The diagnostic worth carrying is exactly that shape. When each round of elimination discharges one instance of a construct by introducing a structurally identical instance one level up, the construct is not reducible — it is the thing everything else is made of, and you are trying to express combination using combination. Recognizing this quickly is what separates productive minimization from the kind that circles. Church is untroubled by the floor because knowing where it lies tells him what a primitive basis has to contain, and he is equally clear that the choice above the floor is often free rather than forced: whether the subtraction sign is a joining notation with no standalone meaning or a name denoting the subtraction function is, he says, an arbitrary decision, and if you are willing to invent a name at all then that name may as well be the sign you were already writing. Syntax versus named value is frequently a labeling preference dressed as an ontological fact.

He also declines a reduction he grants is achievable, which is the other half of the discipline. A prefix scheme in which the operator is written first removes the need for brackets entirely, so the minimal-primitive count can in fact be pushed lower than his own notation reaches. He notes the result, calls its possibility interesting, and keeps the bracketed notation anyway on the grounds that the parenthesis-free form is harder to see the structure of. Minimality is one property among several, and demonstrating that a construct is dispensable does not settle whether to dispense with it. Conversely he keeps joining notations rather than eliminating them where the point of the language is to stay small enough that one branch of the subject can be studied in isolation — a redundant construct earns its place when the value of the language is the narrowness of its surface.

The transfer for a designer is a pair of habits. First, when you are hunting for the irreducible core of a system, look for the construct whose removal requires a fresh copy of itself: the calling convention, the mechanism that composes two things, the way a request is dispatched. That construct is the base, and effort spent trying to express it in terms of the values it operates on is wasted. Second, keep the minimality question and the adoption question apart. A demonstration that four of your five primitives are definable from the fifth is a real result about the system and, on its own, no argument at all for shipping the one-primitive version. The count is a property to know, not a target to hit.

**Source:** [Introduction to Mathematical Logic](../works/introduction-to-mathematical-logic.md) — the section on improper symbols and connectives, which distinguishes symbols meaningful in isolation from those meaningful only in combination, shows that joining notations can generally be traded for names of their associated functions while the application notation cannot because each elimination demands the next arity's notation, treats the connective-versus-function-name choice for arithmetic signs as arbitrary, and acknowledges the achievable bracket-free prefix notation while rejecting it for legibility.
