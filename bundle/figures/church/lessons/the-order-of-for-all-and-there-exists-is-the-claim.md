# The order in which you alternate 'for every' and 'there is' is the whole content of a guarantee

Church introduces quantifiers as operators applied one at a time to an expression with a free variable, each application closing off one variable and turning an open form one step closer to a complete statement. Because they are applied successively, the order of application is part of the resulting expression, and he stops to make the consequence unmissable. Take a two-variable arithmetic relation, close the second variable existentially and then the first universally, and you have said that each thing has some partner standing in that relation to it. Close them in the opposite order and you have said that one particular thing stands in the relation to everything. He picks an example where the two readings actually differ in truth value, so the distinction cannot be waved off as pedantry, and then points at the case every analyst already knows: continuity and uniform continuity are the same string of symbols differing only in where one quantifier sits relative to another. Whether the witness may be chosen after seeing the argument, or must be fixed in advance to work for all of them, is the entire difference between a local property and a global one.

The general shape is that alternating quantifiers encode a dependency order. Inner existentials may depend on the outer universals that precede them; existentials placed outward cannot depend on anything to their right. That is why moving one quantifier across another does not weaken or strengthen a statement by a matter of degree — it replaces it with a different statement about who gets to know what before choosing. Reading a nested claim, the question to ask at each level is not what is being asserted but what is allowed to vary once the earlier choices are made.

This is the most under-recognized precision failure in engineering prose, because informal English flattens the distinction almost completely. For every request there is a worker that can serve it, versus there is a worker that can serve every request: the first is a scheduling property, the second is a capacity claim, and the same sentence in a design document is routinely read both ways by two people in the same meeting. For each input there is a timeout under which the operation completes, versus there is a timeout under which every input completes — the first is compatible with unbounded latency and thus useless as a service guarantee, and it is what most systems actually provide. For every node there comes a time after which it agrees, versus there comes a time after which every node agrees: eventual consistency in the weak sense against a global convergence point, which is a strictly stronger and much more expensive claim.

The habit worth building is mechanical. When a guarantee, an SLA, a test oracle, or an invariant is stated in words, rewrite it with the quantifiers explicit and in order, then check each existential against what it is permitted to depend on. If the intended meaning needs the witness fixed before the input is seen, say so, because that is the version somebody will build against. And when two candidate readings of the same requirement differ only by a swap, do not pick the convenient one — the swap is exactly where the requirement's cost lives, and the cheap reading is usually the one that fails to be worth anything.

**Source:** [Introduction to Mathematical Logic](../works/introduction-to-mathematical-logic.md) — the section on operators and quantifiers, which presents quantification as successive application of an operator to an open form, contrasts a pair of arithmetic statements differing only in the order of a universal and an existential quantifier and having different truth values, and points to continuity versus uniform continuity as the same contrast in familiar analysis.
