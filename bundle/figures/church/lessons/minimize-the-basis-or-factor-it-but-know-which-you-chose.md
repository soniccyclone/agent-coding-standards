# Minimizing a basis and factoring it by concern are opposite goals, and only the factored one lets you vary a part

Surveying the formulations of the propositional calculus that had accumulated by the 1950s, Church sorts them into two camps pulling in opposite directions. One camp chases economy: fewer primitive connectives, fewer axioms, in the limit a single connective and a single axiom, each axiom independent of the rest. The other camp deliberately spends what the first camp saves. It admits more primitive connectives than necessary, accepts that they are interdefinable, tolerates axioms that are not independent, and takes the cost knowingly — in exchange for a basis in which each connective's contribution is isolated into the axioms that mention it and nowhere else.

Church's judgment is that the expensive arrangement earns its price, and the argument is not aesthetic. Because negation appears in exactly one axiom, you can delete that axiom and the connective with it and be left with a coherent smaller system — the fragment of the logic that owes nothing to the existence of negation at all. Because the negation axioms are separable, you can replace just them and land in the intuitionistic logic, with everything else untouched; swap them back differently and you have the classical one. The relationships among a whole family of logics become visible as edits to one region of the basis. None of this is available in the single-axiom formulation. There, every connective is tangled into the one axiom, so the question "what does this connective contribute" has no local answer and no experiment that would produce one.

The general principle is that minimality and modularity are different virtues that trade against each other, and which one you want depends on whether you intend to hold the system fixed or to vary it. A minimal basis is the right artifact when the system is finished and the interesting questions are about the system as a whole — how small can the foundation be, is any part redundant, what is the irreducible core. A factored basis is the right artifact when you expect to produce variants, compare them, or strip the thing down for a setting where some capability is unwanted. Redundancy in a factored basis is not waste; it is the slack that makes a part removable without disturbing the others. And a non-independent axiom is not a defect to be eliminated on sight, because eliminating it may be exactly what welds two previously separable concerns together.

The mistake this guards against in engineering is the reflex that fewer knobs, fewer modules, fewer configuration surfaces are automatically better. Collapse a feature flag into the code path it guards and you have made the system smaller and made the flagless variant unbuildable. Merge two overlapping interfaces into one minimal interface and you may have destroyed your ability to ship the subset. Deduplicate two similar rules into one general rule and the two now change together forever. Every one of those moves is defensible, but each is a decision to stop varying that dimension, and it should be made deliberately rather than absorbed as a general preference for smallness.

The corollary is that the right question about a basis is not "how small is it" but "along which axes can it be varied, and does the structure make those variations local." Church makes the answer legible by presenting each family member as the same core with one region substituted, so a reader can see at a glance what distinguishes them. A basis that cannot be presented that way may still be correct, and may even be smaller, but it has quietly foreclosed the comparison.

**Source:** [Introduction to Mathematical Logic](../works/introduction-to-mathematical-logic.md) — the historical notes to the chapter on the propositional calculus, contrasting the single-axiom and single-connective formulations aimed at economy with the Hilbert–Bernays formulations that deliberately sacrifice economy and independence of the primitive connectives to separate their roles, and observing that the latter arrangement is what exposes the positive and intuitionistic fragments as edits to the negation axioms alone.
