# No prescribed ordering of steps can produce understanding, so methods teach rather than govern

**Lesson:** A methodology is a claim that the work decomposes into named stages in a fixed order, each terminating in a required document. If what the work actually produces is an understanding held by people, the claim cannot be true, because an understanding has no natural seams and no natural sequence — someone who has it can generate whatever presentation is asked for, in whatever order it is asked for, and the presentations are outputs of the grasp rather than stages of assembling it. Prescribing the order of the outputs does not thereby prescribe the order of the thinking, and mistaking the two is what produces processes that generate every required artifact on schedule while the actual comprehension of the system is nobody's assigned task.

The deeper reason no procedure can be the whole story is that following a procedure is itself something done well or badly. If competent action required rules, there would have to be rules for applying the rules, and rules for those, without end. Skill is what stops the regress, which means every method rests on judgment it cannot supply and cannot be evaluated as if it did. The same argument dissolves the appeal to scientific method as a model: the notion that working scientists succeed by executing a general procedure does not survive contact with how discovery actually goes, so importing it as the justification for a software process imports an assumption that was never sound.

None of this makes accumulated technique worthless, and the distinction matters for how you use it. Familiarity with model solutions to recurring problems, with ways of describing and checking designs, and with principles for structuring systems of many interacting parts is exactly what raises the quality of the understanding someone can form. Treat that material as curriculum — things worth knowing, drawn on when the problem calls for them, applied in any order — rather than as a schedule. And notice how weak the evidence for the schedule interpretation is: claimed successes of particular methods are largely uncontrolled reports, since the controlled comparison is expensive and the concepts involved are hard to pin down operationally, so what looks like a track record is mostly advocacy.

**Source:** [Programming as Theory Building](../works/programming-as-theory-building.md) — the section on method and theory building: the mismatch between a method's ordered actions and a theory's lack of inherent parts or ordering, the infinite regress that rules out rule-following as the basis of intelligent action, the rejection of the scientific-method analogy, the absence of controlled studies behind method success claims, and the conclusion that what survives is the educational effect.
