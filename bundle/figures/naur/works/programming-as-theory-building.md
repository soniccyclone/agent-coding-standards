description: Argues that the real product of programming is not the code itself but a theory the programmers hold of how the problem maps onto the running program — a grasp that lets them explain design choices, extend the system, and fix it under changing demands. The paper draws on Ryle's distinction between knowing-how and knowing-that to ground the claim epistemically rather than just anecdotally.
