description: Argues that the real product of programming is not the code itself but a theory the programmers hold of how the problem maps onto the running program — a grasp that lets them explain design choices, extend the system, and fix it under changing demands. Naur uses this to explain why programs decay once the people who built that theory leave and only the text remains, and why documentation alone cannot substitute for it. The paper draws on Ryle's distinction between knowing-how and knowing-that to ground the claim epistemically rather than just anecdotally.
