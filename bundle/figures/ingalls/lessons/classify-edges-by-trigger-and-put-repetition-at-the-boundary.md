# A drawn cycle is not a causal one: classify each link by what triggers it, and keep repetition at the boundary of an acyclic region

**Lesson:** An acyclic dependency structure is worth defending hard, because acyclicity is what makes a structure readable without reference to time and orderable ahead of execution instead of searched at run time. The mistake is to conclude from the presence of a closed path in the picture that acyclicity has been lost. Ask instead what causes each link to carry a value. An element that emits when something outside stimulates it — a person, a device, a clock — is not emitting in response to its inputs, so it cannot close a chain of causation no matter how the diagram is drawn. The path closes on paper; the dependency does not. This gives a general diagnostic worth applying wherever feedback appears to be present: classify links by trigger rather than by topology, and the apparent cycles usually resolve into an acyclic dependency structure with one or more externally driven sources sitting inside it. What you must not do is accept the drawing's verdict, because that either forces you into general machinery you do not need or leaves you believing your model is more time-dependent than it is.

The genuine need for repetition is a separate matter and takes a separate answer: put the repetition at the boundary of the acyclic region rather than as a back edge inside it. A construct at the edge admits an aggregate, feeds the interior one element at a time, runs the interior to completion per element, and collects the results as they come out. Every pass through the interior remains order-free and orderable in advance, exactly as before, and the interior needs no notion of which pass it is in. Only the boundary construct knows about repetition, which makes it the one place that has to handle the two ideas that genuinely involve time: values that persist from one pass to the next, and the condition that stops the process. Concentrating those two in a named place, rather than letting them appear implicitly wherever a back edge was drawn, is what keeps the interior's guarantees intact — and it is fine for the number of passes to depend on the arriving data, since that is the boundary's business and not the interior's.

The general form of this is a rule about where to put the parts of a model that resist your preferred discipline. Every simple, strong discipline has a few things it cannot express, and there is always a choice between weakening the discipline everywhere and confining the exceptions to explicit constructs at the edges. Weakening is cheaper to implement and vastly more expensive to live with, since it converts every question about the model into a question about time and sequencing. Confinement costs a construct with real semantics that has to be designed carefully and understood by users, and buys back a large interior in which nobody has to think about ordering at all. Prefer confinement, and be suspicious of any diagnosis of your model's shape that was made from its picture rather than from what actually triggers what.

**Source:** [Fabrik: A Visual Programming Environment](../works/fabrik-a-visual-programming-environment.md) — the bidirectionality section's insistence on a loop-free and therefore timeless, declarative model; the slider walkthrough's argument that the closed path through the pointer component is not a violation of that model because the pointer's output is triggered by user action rather than by data arriving at its inputs; and the iteration section, where boundary gateways disaggregate an incoming collection, fire the interior once per element, and reaggregate the results, with strict scoping inside each pass, state carried between passes held by the gateways, the pass count determined by topology and actual data, and terminator and pass-count gateways supplied as explicit parts of the boundary vocabulary.
