# Dan Ingalls

**Dates:** b. 1944. Chief architect/implementer of successive Smalltalk versions at Xerox PARC, later led Squeak at Apple/Disney and co-created the Lively Kernel.

## Why a candidate
Wrote the canonical statement of Smalltalk's design principles and was the primary engineer who made "live, explorable environment" a working reality rather than a slogan.

## Top 10 most influential works
Only ~6-7 genuinely distinct, individually citable works — much of his output is embedded in collaborative system releases:
1. "Design Principles Behind Smalltalk" (1981, Byte) — `public` (multiple mirrors: worrydream.com, gwern.net)
2. "Back to the Future: The Story of Squeak" (1997, with Kay, Wallace et al.) — `public` (self-archived on Squeak project sites)
3. "The Lively Kernel: A Self-Supporting System on a Web Page" (2008, with Palacz et al.) — `public` (Lively Kernel project pages)
4. "The Smalltalk-76 Programming System Design and Implementation" (1978, POPL) — `paywalled`
5. "Fabrik: A Visual Programming Environment" (1988, OOPSLA) — `paywalled`
6. "A Simple Technique for Handling Multiple Polymorphism" (1986, OOPSLA) — `paywalled`

## Phase 3 verification (works/)
All six works above were re-verified. Items 4-6 were flagged `paywalled` in the earlier
pass but turned out to have legitimate open copies (Internet Archive bitsavers, Computer
History Museum, worrydream.com, a university course bibliography mirror) — all six are now
formalized as `work` files under `works/`, five confirmed public.

## Phase 3 access flag
"The Lively Kernel: A Self-Supporting System on a Web Page" (2008, with Palacz, Uhler,
Taivalsaari, Mikkonen; Workshop on Self-Sustaining Systems, Springer LNCS 5146) is
genuinely unavailable in public form and was dropped from the works/ list rather than
formalized. This is one of the two pillars of Ingalls' "why a candidate" case (the other
being Smalltalk itself) — the Lively Kernel is his most direct proof that the "live,
explorable environment" idea ports to the browser. Checked: the publisher link
(link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/978-3-540-89275-5_2.pdf) redirects to a Springer
IdP login wall (303 to idp.springer.com/authorize); the Lively Kernel project's own
publications page (lively-kernel.org/publications/index.html) lists this paper but provides
no direct link, only links for other, later Lively-family papers; the S3 2008 workshop site
(hpi.uni-potsdam.de/hirschfeld/s3/s3-08/) points back to the same Springer proceedings with
no self-archived copy; Google Scholar shows no open-access variant; no Wayback Machine
snapshot exists for the Springer PDF URL. A tangentially related paper on the same system
(Palacz, "The Lively Kernel Web Application Framework," SVG Open 2008) was found in search
results but its host domain (svgopen.org) has since been squatted by an unrelated site, and
Ingalls is not an author on it — not a valid substitute. No further action taken; flagging
per standing procedure rather than substituting a lesser work.
