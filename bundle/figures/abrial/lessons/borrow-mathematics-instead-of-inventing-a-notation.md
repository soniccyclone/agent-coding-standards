# The urge to invent a language is a programmer's reflex; mathematics already has the vocabulary, and the design belongs in a structure rather than a text

**Lesson:** Faced with a domain to describe, people trained as programmers reach for a new notation, because designing notations is what they know how to do. Other disciplines that model things do not behave this way: physics and operations research describe their subjects in set theory and predicate logic and have never felt the lack. The reflex costs more than it looks. A fresh notation arrives with no established meaning, so its semantics have to be defined, argued about, and tooled from scratch, and every reader must learn it before they can disagree with anything expressed in it. Sets, relations, functions and first-order quantification are already there, already understood, already equipped with a body of results, and they are indifferent to which domain you point them at. The related refusal is just as important: do not hide the mathematics from the engineers who must read the model. An engineer who works with fields and impedances is not going to be frightened by a quantifier, and the assumption that they will is condescension dressed as pragmatism.

There is a second half to this, which is about the medium the design is stored in rather than the vocabulary it is written in. The instinct that a formal development is a text file — because programs are text files — is another borrowed reflex, and it caps what tools can do for you. A development consists of variables, invariants, events, guards, actions, refinement layers, obligations and proofs, together with the relationships among them: which invariant a given obligation came from, which layer a variable was introduced in, which proofs depend on which hypotheses. Held as a structure over those objects rather than as a linear document, the mechanization becomes possible that matters most in practice — determining exactly which proofs a small model change invalidates, rather than redoing all of them. That single capability decides whether a proved development can be revised at industrial speed, and it is unavailable to anything that treats the model as prose.

The unifying idea is that a design's representation is not a stylistic matter. It sets an upper bound on what can be reasoned about, by people and by machines, and both bounds are usually decided by accident. A programmer who takes this seriously asks what already exists before minting vocabulary, and asks what mechanical help a representation permits before choosing to store the design as text.

**Source:** [Faultless Systems: Yes We Can!](../works/faultless-systems-yes-we-can.md) — the passages arguing that inventing modeling languages is a mistake computer scientists in particular make, rejecting the idea that mathematical notation must be concealed, and proposing that tooling be built over a structured repository of modeling objects rather than over formal texts. Also [Formal Methods in Industry: Achievements, Problems, Future](../works/formal-methods-in-industry-achievements-problems-future.md) — its argument that using something language-shaped to hold the refinement layers is the wrong approach, and that differential proof management needs a navigable store instead. Also [Data Semantics](../works/data-semantics.md) — built on borrowed axiomatic set theory and existing language notions, and closing by disclaiming any intention to propose a language.
