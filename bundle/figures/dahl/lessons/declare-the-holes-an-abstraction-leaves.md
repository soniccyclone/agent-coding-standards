# State the holes: everything a general design leaves to its specializations should be a short declared list, and nothing else should be reachable

**Lesson:** A general mechanism that expects to be specialized has two kinds of relationship with the specializations that will be written later. There are the decisions it deliberately defers — the comparison, the placement rule, the encoding — and there is everything else, which the later author must not be able to disturb. Most reuse mechanisms conflate these: the general code can call anything the specialization defines, the specialization can see and shadow anything the general code declares, and the resulting dependency runs in both directions over the whole surface. Then nobody can change either side, because nobody can enumerate what the other side is relying on. The alternative is to make the deferred decisions explicit and finite. Declare, by name, the quantities the general text intends to call but does not intend to fix; supply a working version of each so the general text is complete and usable on its own; and let a specialization substitute one of them without touching anything else.

The same discipline extends to control, which is where it becomes surprising. The general layer normally has a skeleton — set up, then do the specific thing, then finish — and if specializations are simply appended after it, the skeleton cannot wrap them. So let the general text mark the position where the specialized statements belong. The consequence inverts the usual intuition about who is in charge: the outer, more general layer owns the sequence, and the inner, more specific layer supplies the contents of a slot. What appears in a language definition as a mundane placeholder is really the statement that a framework may keep its invariants across the code that customizes it.

Two properties make this pay rather than merely tidy. First, the general layer's dependence on its unwritten specializations is now a written-down list, short enough to read, which is the only form in which such a dependence can be maintained. Second, the complement is enforced: names that were not declared as holes are systematically kept out of reach, so a specialization that happens to introduce a name the general text already uses cannot capture it, and adding a field to a general definition cannot silently rewire code beneath it. A programmer who works this way treats "which of my decisions am I deferring, and to whom" as a design artifact to be published, and regards a reuse mechanism that lets subordinate code reach arbitrarily into its superior as a source of coupling he will not be able to pay off later.

Also [Class and Subclass Declarations](../works/class-and-subclass-declarations.md) — the extension sections introducing split bodies and virtual quantities, together with the discussion afterwards where Dahl explains that a collision between a declared and a virtual name establishes identity while any other collision is an ordinary redeclaration error.
