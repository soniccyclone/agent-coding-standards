# Make restart, rollback and task-switching operators over a component that knows nothing about them

**Lesson:** Operational capabilities are normally written into the thing that needs them: a game learns about its own reset key, a database learns about its own snapshots, a service learns about its own retries, and each acquires a tangle of state that exists only to support the facility. Write the component without any of it, then define restarting, snapshot-and-rollback, and switch-away-and-resume as transformations applied from outside. Each transformation introduces one new occurrence that the component's own vocabulary does not contain, and gives that occurrence meaning by saying what the whole assembly becomes when it happens. The component is untouched and unaware. The condition doing the real work is that the new occurrence lies outside the component's vocabulary, because that is exactly what guarantees the component can neither trigger it nor interfere with it.

Each capability then reduces to one or two equations about what the assembly becomes after the new occurrence, and those equations are the entire specification. Catastrophe: afterwards the whole becomes a designated recovery. Restart: afterwards the whole becomes itself, from the beginning. Checkpointing: after a mark, the whole becomes itself with the present state remembered; after a fault, it becomes whatever was remembered, or the beginning if nothing was. Alternation between two activities: after the switch, the whole becomes the pair with the roles exchanged and each side's progress preserved. It is worth pausing on how much is settled by how little — and on the one subtlety, which is that such an equation usually fails to pin the operator down on its own, since something permitting absolutely everything satisfies it too. You must additionally say you mean the smallest such thing.

Two payoffs justify the discipline. The capabilities stack, because each is an operator producing the same kind of object it consumes: a restartable thing can be made checkpointable, an alternating pair can be made restartable, and the matrix of which components support which facilities collapses from a decision made per component into one made per assembly. And the specification stays tiny while the implementation absorbs all the difficulty — a rollback is defined in a line and implemented by sharing as much structure as possible between the live state and the saved one, an optimization that is thoroughly machine- and application-specific and can be replaced wholesale without the definition moving. That is the separation worth engineering for: the capability defined once in terms nobody can misread, with the implementation left free to be as devious as the hardware rewards.

**Source:** [Communicating Sequential Processes](../works/communicating-sequential-processes-book.md) — the interrupts section of the sequential processes chapter: the interrupt operator, whose laws make it associative with the stopped process as unit and require the interrupting event to lie outside the interrupted process's alphabet; the catastrophe operator built from it, defined by a single law saying what the combination becomes after the catastrophic event; the restartable process defined as a guarded recursion over that operator, with the remark that a game is best defined independently of the restart facility and then transformed; the alternation operator specified by laws exchanging the two activities on a switch event while preserving each one's progress, suggested as a facility for switching between utilities without losing one's place; and the checkpointing operator, specified by two laws and accompanied by the observation that an implementor shares as much data as possible between current and checkpoint states, an optimization that is highly machine and application dependent while the mathematics remains simple.
