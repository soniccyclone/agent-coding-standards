# A foundation meant to host many theories must distinguish as much as possible; a tool meant to be used must identify as much as possible

**Lesson:** When you build a representation, you choose how much of the difference between two things it is allowed to erase. That choice looks like a question of taste or precision, and it is neither: it is determined by whether the representation is meant to be built on or to be used, and the two answers point in opposite directions with no compromise between them.

If your representation is intended as a substrate for a family of more specific theories, each of which will decide for itself which differences do not matter, then you must not decide any of that in advance. Every identification you make is one that no theory built on top can undo — a downstream theory can always merge two things you kept apart, but nothing can recover a distinction you already discarded. Generality therefore obliges you to distinguish everything not absolutely forced to be equal, and to accept the consequence: because your notion of sameness is so fine, very few general statements about it are true, so the substrate itself supports almost no useful rewriting. That is not a defect. It is what makes the substrate hospitable.

If instead the representation is meant to be reasoned with directly — to justify replacing a design by an implementation, or a component by a cheaper one — the pressure runs entirely the other way. Every distinction you preserve is a pair of things you may not interchange, so distinctions are exactly what cost you laws, and laws are what you came for. The move is to identify aggressively, keeping only the differences that some property you care about actually depends on, and to accept the matching consequence: your theory is now committed, cannot serve as a neutral platform for someone whose interests differ, and will need rework if the set of properties you care about grows. Both designs are correct, and the mistake is not picking one but failing to notice you picked. A general-purpose foundation being criticized for supporting no useful reasoning, or a purpose-built theory being criticized for not accommodating someone else's question, are both criticisms of a design succeeding at what it was for. The practical form of this: before arguing about whether two things should count as the same, establish whether you are building the floor or the tool, because that settles the argument and nothing else does.

**Source:** [Communicating Sequential Processes](../works/communicating-sequential-processes-book.md) — the CCS comparison in the discussion chapter's mathematical-models section, where the worked examples showing that internal-step nondeterminism is not associative and that prefixing does not distribute through it are used to observe that CCS distinguishes many processes the book identifies; with the reason given that CCS is intended as a framework for a family of models, each of which may make more identifications but cannot make fewer, so it makes only the identifications that seem absolutely essential — whereas the book's model pursues the opposite goal of making as many identifications as possible while preserving only the most essential distinctions, and thereby obtains a far richer set of algebraic laws permitting more transformations and optimisations.
