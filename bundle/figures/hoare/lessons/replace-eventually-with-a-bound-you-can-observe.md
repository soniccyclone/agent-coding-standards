# Replace "eventually" with a bound, because no finite observation can ever refute an unbounded promise

**Lesson:** A requirement that something will happen eventually, with no limit on how long it may be postponed, has a fatal property: no observation of finite length can distinguish a system that is going to satisfy it from one that never will. Watch as long as you like; the thing has not happened yet; that is consistent with both. Such a requirement therefore cannot be tested, cannot be monitored, and cannot be violated in any way you could point at — which means in practice that it is not a requirement at all but an expression of hope. The repair is to state a bound. Not "the request is eventually served" but "no run of more than n steps fails to contain a service" — and then the system has to be built so that the bound is actually enforced, typically by carrying an explicit count of how much deferral has already occurred and removing the option to defer once the count is used up.

That last point is where the discipline earns its keep, because it changes the artifact and not merely the wording. An unbounded promise imposes no obligation on any component; it can be signed off by everybody and delivered by nobody. A bound must be implemented somewhere, by something that keeps score, and the moment you try to write it down you discover which component is in a position to keep that score and whether it has the information it needs. Requirements that survive the rewrite tend to be genuine; requirements that cannot be given a bound by anyone are usually revealed to be someone else's problem, or nobody's, and either way that is worth finding out during design rather than during an incident.

There is a second and independent separation worth keeping. Some of what people want from "eventually" is really a probabilistic claim — that a party which could be served infinitely often will not be starved forever by an unlucky pattern of choices. That is a legitimate thing to want and it is not a logical property; it depends on the distribution of choices, not on what is possible. Keeping it out of the correctness argument and handling it as its own concern, with its own vocabulary, keeps the correctness argument finite and checkable, and stops probability from being smuggled in as an assumption that the underlying model does not license. The general form of the rule: any obligation you cannot express in the terms your model actually observes should be named, stated in its own terms, and assigned to a definite layer — never left implicit in the hope that whoever implements the thing will be reasonable.

**Source:** [Communicating Sequential Processes](../works/communicating-sequential-processes-book.md) — the nondeterministic-choice section of the nondeterminism chapter, which declines to build fairness into the theory on the grounds that only finite traces are observed, so an event that may be postponed indefinitely can never be known to be coming; requires instead that insisting on an event means asserting a number n such that every trace longer than n contains it, with an accompanying process definition that counts down deferrals and removes the alternative once the limit is reached; and recommends that fairness, if wanted, be specified and implemented at a separate stage by ascribing nonzero probabilities to the alternatives, keeping probabilistic reasoning apart from concerns about logical correctness.
