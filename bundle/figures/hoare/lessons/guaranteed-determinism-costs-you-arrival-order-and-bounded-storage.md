# A model that guarantees determinism pays for it in storage it cannot bound and in questions it cannot ask about arrival order

**Lesson:** A concurrency model in which every component is a function from its input streams to its output streams has an attractive headline property: the result of the whole network is fixed regardless of scheduling, so testing means something, reruns reproduce, and there is no interleaving space to search. That property is not free, and its price is worth itemizing before adopting the model, because the invoice arrives in three places at once and none of them is where you were looking.

It arrives first in storage. If a component's output is a stream that its consumers read at their own pace, and no consumer may be made to wait on another, then produced values must be held until every consumer has taken them, and nothing in the model bounds how far ahead a producer may run. Unbounded buffering is not an implementation weakness to be optimized away later; it is what makes the semantics come out deterministic, and every scheme for bounding it is a partial retreat from the guarantee. It arrives second in expressiveness, and this is the cut that hurts. A component cannot ask to proceed on whichever of two inputs arrives first, because the answer would depend on timing and timing is precisely what has been abstracted out. Any behaviour whose whole point is responsiveness to arrival order — serving whoever is ready, merging streams fairly, abandoning a slow source for a fast one — is not slow in this model, it is unsayable. Third, it arrives as the absence of an escape hatch: since determinism is a property of the model rather than of any one program, a single component that genuinely needs to make a timing-dependent choice cannot be accommodated without weakening the guarantee everywhere.

The generalizable point is about how to evaluate any model that offers a strong global property. Do not compare it against the alternatives on the programs you already know how to write in it — those are the ones it was designed for and it will win. Compare it on the things it forbids, and separate the two kinds of forbidding: what merely becomes clumsy, and what becomes inexpressible. Clumsiness is a cost you can pay repeatedly; inexpressibility is a wall, and hitting it late means the guarantee has to be given up after the architecture has been built on it. The honest form of the choice is to write down, in advance, the arrival-order-dependent behaviours your system will eventually need, and then decide whether determinism is worth not having them.

**Source:** [Communicating Sequential Processes](../works/communicating-sequential-processes-book.md) — the functional-network subsection of the discussion chapter's communication section, which after developing cyclic networks as sets of mutually recursive equations (illustrated by the Hamming-numbers problem built from multiplier processes feeding a merge) lists the four respects in which the functional approach to multiprocessor networks differs from the book's: a general implementation requires unbounded buffering on all channels, each buffered value must be retained until every inputting process has taken it, there is no possibility for a process to wait for whichever of two inputs arrives first, and all processes are deterministic — with the note that research was then directed at reducing the first two inefficiencies and relaxing the last two restrictions.
