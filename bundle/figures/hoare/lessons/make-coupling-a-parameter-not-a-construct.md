# Make coupling a parameter of the participants, not a choice of construct

**Lesson:** The obvious way to provide concurrency is two facilities: one for running things that have nothing to do with each other, and one for making things happen together. They get separate constructs, separate implementations and separate reasoning, and the enormous middle ground — participants that are mostly independent and interact at a few points — has to be assembled by hand out of both. There is a better factoring. Provide one joining operator, and let its behaviour be determined entirely by how much the participants' declared vocabularies overlap. Anything named by both must happen with both taking part, as a single occurrence; anything named by only one happens unilaterally, with the other unable to notice or prevent it. Disjoint vocabularies then yield exactly free interleaving. Identical vocabularies yield exactly lockstep agreement. Every intermediate degree of coupling is available for free, with no new construct and no new proof, because it was never a different mechanism.

The economies are larger than they look. Every law about the operator is written once and holds across the entire range, so the awkward middle cases — which is where real systems live — inherit the reasoning that was developed on the easy extremes rather than needing their own. The degree of coupling stops being a property of the joining machinery and becomes a declaration attached to each participant, which means it is adjusted by editing a name rather than by restructuring the composition. And the two supposedly opposite regimes are revealed as the same thing at two settings of a control nobody had previously named, which is usually a sign that the factoring is right rather than merely convenient.

Generalize the move: when a system offers two mechanisms that feel like opposites, look for the quantity that would have to vary continuously to turn one into the other. Often it exists, often it is a set rather than a number, and making it explicit collapses two mechanisms into one while making the space between them constructible instead of hand-rolled. Then pay attention to what the collapse relocates, because it always relocates something. Here, coupling now lives in the choice of names, so two components that happen to use one name for unrelated purposes are silently welded together, and a component cannot be reused next to a stranger without checking the overlap. That promotes systematic renaming from a cosmetic facility to a load-bearing one: the moment identity of names is what determines interaction, the ability to rename on the way into a composition is the only thing keeping components independently developable.

**Source:** [Communicating Sequential Processes](../works/communicating-sequential-processes-book.md) — the interaction and concurrency sections of the concurrency chapter, where the parallel operator is first defined for participants sharing one vocabulary as requiring simultaneous participation in every event, then generalized to differing vocabularies so that events private to one participant occur without the other being able to control or notice them, with the resulting vocabulary being the union; together with the closing summary that traces of the combination are interleavings in which shared events occur once, degenerating to pure interleaving when the vocabularies are disjoint and to intersection when they coincide.
