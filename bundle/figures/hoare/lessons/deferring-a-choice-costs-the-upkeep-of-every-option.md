# Deferring a choice costs the upkeep of every option you kept open, for as long as they stay indistinguishable

**Lesson:** When a design permits either of two behaviours, there are three positions available. Commit now on the grounds you have. Commit when the thing is built, on grounds available then. Or refuse to commit and let circumstances force the choice at the last possible moment. The third is strictly the safest with respect to getting stuck: if either option would have worked, waiting until the world indicates which one is being demanded will find it, whereas an early commitment can pick the one that fails in the situation that actually arises. This looks like a free win and is not. Holding an option open means keeping it alive — until the alternatives become distinguishable you must maintain every one of them and advance them in step — so the cost is proportional to how long they remain indistinguishable. In the degenerate case where they never diverge, you pay for all of them forever and receive nothing, since there was never anything to choose between.

That gives a usable test instead of a preference. Before adopting the deferring strategy, ask two quantities: how long the alternatives stay indistinguishable in the typical case rather than the worst one, and what carrying each of them costs per unit of that time. Deferral is excellent when the discriminating information arrives almost at once and the options are cheap to hold, and ruinous when the discriminator is distant or never comes. Notice how many familiar decisions are this same trade in different units — speculating past a branch, keeping a migration reversible, running two implementations behind a switch, declining to fix a data layout until the access pattern is known. Each is a bet on how soon the world will tell you which option you needed, priced against what it costs to hold both meanwhile.

The remaining question is what you owe when you decline to defer, and the answer is: an argument, not an intention. The deferring strategy exists to avoid a failure that early commitment can cause, so choosing early means claiming that failure cannot occur. Often that claim is cheap to establish — if both options succeed in every situation the system can actually reach, take either, take it now, and keep the symmetry in the description while the artifact enjoys the asymmetry. Where the claim cannot be established, the two honest moves are to defer and pay for it, or to redesign so that the situation in which the choice matters cannot arise. Committing early while quietly relying on the bad case never showing up is the only indefensible position, because it takes on the risk of having no strategy while paying for the cheap one.
