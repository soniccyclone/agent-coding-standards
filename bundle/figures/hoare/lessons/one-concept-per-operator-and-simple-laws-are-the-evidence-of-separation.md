# Give each operator exactly one concept, and treat the simplicity of its laws as the evidence that the concepts really were separate

**Lesson:** There are two defensible ways to build a vocabulary of combining forms, and they optimize for different work. One is to minimize the number of primitives, taking as your basic combinator something powerful enough that the other operations fall out as special cases. A single concurrency operator can be made to carry synchronization, hiding, nondeterministic resolution of competing partners and independent interleaving all at once, and everything else is then derived. This is the right choice when your subject is the space of theories itself, because a small basis is a small thing to vary, and results about it transfer to every derived notion for free. The other way is to fix one simple underlying model and then define as many operators over it as there are distinct ideas to name, accepting a larger vocabulary in exchange for each element of it meaning one thing.

The second is usually the better choice for anyone who has to reason about actual designs, and the reason is worth stating precisely: what a practitioner manipulates is not the operator set, it is the algebraic laws. Laws are how a design gets rearranged into an implementation, how a compound gets replaced by a cheaper equivalent, how an optimization is justified. A vocabulary in which one operator bundles four ideas has laws that mention four ideas at once, and each such law is harder to state, harder to remember, and applicable in fewer situations. Split the ideas — pure internal choice separate from choice offered to the environment, concurrent composition separate from concealment, concealment separate from nondeterminism — and each operator's laws become short, because a law only has to talk about the one thing its operator does. Fewer primitives is a real economy, but it is an economy in the definition, and it is paid for out of the reasoning that happens millions of times afterwards.

The genuinely useful part is the diagnostic hiding in that observation. If you have separated your operators correctly, the laws come out simple; if the laws stay complicated, you have not yet cut the concepts at their joints, and some operator is still carrying more than one idea. That gives you a test you can apply to your own design rather than an aesthetic preference you have to defend. When two things really are independent, statements about one of them do not need to mention the other, and the algebra shows this immediately. So the sequence is: guess at the decomposition, define the operators, write the laws, and let the untidiness of the laws tell you where the guess was wrong. Notice too that the tension between the two approaches is not a disagreement about the subject matter — it is a disagreement about who the vocabulary is for, and the honest form of the choice is to say which audience you are serving rather than to argue that the other basis is malformed.

**Source:** [Communicating Sequential Processes](../works/communicating-sequential-processes-book.md) — the CCS comparison in the discussion chapter's section on mathematical models: the account of the single CCS parallel combinator as bundling hiding, nondeterminism and interleaving along with synchronisation, so that no separate concealment operator is needed; the observation that maximum expressive power from as few primitive operators as possible is the source of CCS's elegance and simplifies the investigation of families of models; and Hoare's statement of the complementary approach taken in the book, seeking simplicity through a single simple model over which as many operators are defined as there are distinct concepts to investigate, with nondeterministic choice independent of environmental choice and concurrency independent of nondeterminism and hiding, together with the remark that the simplicity of the corresponding algebraic laws is what indicates the concepts are genuinely distinct, and that a wide operator range suits practical application while operator minimisation suits theoretical investigation.
