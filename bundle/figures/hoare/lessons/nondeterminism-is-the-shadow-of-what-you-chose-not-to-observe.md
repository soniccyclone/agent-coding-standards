# Nondeterminism in a model is the shadow of what you chose not to observe, which makes it a control rather than an affliction

**Lesson:** When a description says either of two things may happen and offers no rule for which, people reach for one of two explanations: the world is genuinely capricious here, or the description is sloppy. Usually neither. The unpredictability was manufactured by the modeller at the moment they fixed which occurrences the model is allowed to mention. Whatever actually settles the outcome — how the machine was loaded, which participant the scheduler favoured, what was already in the cache — is entirely definite; it is simply not among the things the description talks about, so inside the description it cannot be consulted, and the outcome has to be reported as arbitrary. Nondeterminism is therefore derived rather than intrinsic. It is the exact residue of the abstraction you chose, and there is nothing mysterious in it.

Reading it that way converts it from an affliction into a dial. Too much arbitrariness in your model means you excluded something you turn out to need: the fix is to admit that occurrence into the vocabulary and watch the arbitrariness disappear, paid for with a larger model that now has to discuss loading, scheduling or caching. Too little means you have committed to details that will not hold, and the reasoning you did is valid only for the specific circumstances you accidentally described. So the modelling question is not whether the system is deterministic but how much unpredictability you wish to buy and what you are paying in vocabulary to get it — a decision to be made deliberately, whose right setting genuinely differs between two models of the same system built for different purposes. Keeping the arbitrariness is what lets a description stay at a level where a human can hold it.

Two consequences for practice. When a real system behaves unpredictably, the productive first question is which factor your description omits, because that names the thing to instrument if determinism is what you actually need — and the factor is frequently one that somebody abstracted away deliberately, long ago, in a document nobody has read since. And when you do choose to leave a factor out, record that you chose it. The difference between "we abstracted from the load order" and "we never considered the load order" is invisible in the finished description and enormous in what it entitles a reader to conclude: an arbitrary choice accompanied by a stated exclusion is a licence granted to the implementor, while the same arbitrary choice with no such note is an unexamined hole that somebody will later fill in with whatever the code happens to do.

**Source:** [Communicating Sequential Processes](../works/communicating-sequential-processes-book.md) — the introduction to the nondeterminism chapter, which distinguishes choices the environment can make or at least observe from choices made internally, where the environment can neither control nor observe the selection nor tell when it occurred though it may infer it afterwards from what follows; and states that there is nothing mysterious about the latter kind, since it arises from a deliberate decision to ignore the factors influencing the selection — the example being a change-giving machine whose combination depends on how it was loaded with coins, those events having been excluded from its alphabet — concluding that nondeterminism is what keeps descriptions of physical systems at a high level of abstraction.
