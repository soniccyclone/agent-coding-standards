# Hidden internal activity that never finishes is indistinguishable from being hung, so hiding obliges you to bound it

**Lesson:** Once a mechanism has been assembled from parts, the traffic between those parts is nobody's business outside, and hiding it is exactly what makes the assembly usable as a single component. That hiding carries a consequence that is rarely priced. A hidden step, being invisible, is also uncontrollable and untimed: it occurs on its own, as soon as it can, with nothing outside able to gate it. So if the assembly can perform an unbounded run of hidden steps, then from outside it is doing nothing at all, forever, and no observation distinguishes it from a component that has locked up or from a definition that never meant anything in the first place. The strength of that claim is the whole point. It is not that the cases are hard to tell apart; it is that they cannot be, because the vocabulary that would have separated them is precisely what you removed.

Offering a visible alternative does not rescue you. A component that can either take a hidden step or accept a request from outside cannot promise the request will ever be accepted, since nothing stops it taking the hidden step every time the opportunity arises and there is no notion of fairness to appeal to unless one was deliberately built in. The mirror image holds too, and it is the useful half: if the environment is not ready for the visible alternative, the hidden step must be taken, so internal work does make progress whenever nothing external is competing for the same moment. Both halves say one thing. Hidden activity has a priority you never granted it, because in hiding it you destroyed the means by which priority could have been granted or withheld.

The rule that follows is concrete. Whenever you hide internal activity behind an interface, you have taken on an obligation to bound it: between any two externally visible events the amount of hidden work must be finite, and something must be answerable for making that so. That obligation is the real content of the folklore about livelock, retry storms, cache thrashing and collection pauses behind a facade — every one of them is unbounded hidden activity, and every one of them presents to a client as a component that has simply stopped. There is a corollary for operations. A component that is hung and a component that is furiously busy internally produce the same observation from outside, so any interface you expect to run in earnest must expose something that separates them. That exposure is a deliberate hole cut in the abstraction, not a leak to be tidied up later, and deciding its size is part of designing the interface rather than an afterthought.

**Source:** [Communicating Sequential Processes](../works/communicating-sequential-processes-book.md) — the concealment section of the nondeterminism chapter: the statement that concealed events are intended to occur automatically and instantaneously as soon as they can without the environment's knowledge or intervention; the derivation showing that concealing an unbounded sequence of consecutive internal events yields the same outcome as an infinite loop or unguarded recursion, given the name divergence; the second derivation showing the same result even when an unconcealed alternative is infinitely often on offer, with the remark that nothing prevents the process from choosing the hidden event every time and that this is related to the earlier decision not to insist on fairness; the counterbalancing observation that when the environment is not ready for the visible alternative the hidden event must occur; and the implementation subsection, where applying the hiding function to such a process recurses without exit so that no further communication with the outside world ever occurs.
