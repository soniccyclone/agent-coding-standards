# A restricted combinator earns its keep with a simpler law — and trades one hazard for another you must name

**Lesson:** The general way of joining components is powerful, and its reasoning rule drags along every complication the general case can produce. Define a restricted form instead — each participant has exactly one connection, in a fixed direction, and the connection is hidden — and something disproportionate happens to the mathematics. Two parties joined by a single connection cannot fail to agree with each other, so the whole question of mutual blockage vanishes from the rule, and what remains is ordinary relational composition: the relation the assembly maintains between its outer ends is the composition of the two component relations, with the intermediate history existentially quantified and thereafter forgotten. A rule that required the full apparatus becomes one an engineer applies in their head.

That is the genuine argument for a specialized combinator, and it is not convenience. A restricted form is worth introducing when its law is simpler than the general law, not when it merely shortens what you type. The test is concrete and worth running before adopting one: write out what must be proved to compose two things in the general way, write out what must be proved in the restricted way, and check whether terms actually disappeared. If nothing disappeared you have acquired a notation plus an obligation to show it agrees with the general form, and gained nothing. If something disappeared, that thing is the hazard the restriction eliminates by construction, and it should be named explicitly, because it is the entire reason the restriction exists and the first thing someone will try to relax.

Then go looking for what the restriction did not remove, because hazards are traded far more often than eliminated. Joining through exactly one connection abolishes mutual blockage and immediately admits its cousin: two stages conversing with each other forever and with the outside world never again. That failure needs a certificate of its own, and the certificate is a productivity bound — no stage may emit without limit unless it also consumes — which can be read off the text whenever every self-reference within a stage sits behind a consumption. There is an economy here worth taking away: only one of the two stages needs the property for the pair to be safe, so the obligation attaches to a component once rather than to every composition it will ever appear in. A structural hazard removed by the shape of the connection, plus a residual hazard discharged once per component, is what makes long chains tractable at all.

**Source:** [Communicating Sequential Processes](../works/communicating-sequential-processes-book.md) — the pipes section of the communication chapter: the livelock subsection, which observes that chaining connects two processes by just one channel and so introduces no risk of deadlock, then exhibits pairs that communicate with each other endlessly while never again communicating externally, notes that this arises from the mere possibility of unbounded internal communication even when external alternatives are offered at every step, defines a process as left-guarded when the length of its output is bounded by a function of its input, states that this follows when every recursion is guarded by an input, and concludes that either operand having the property suffices; and the specifications subsection, whose composition rule is the relational composition of the two operands' relations over an existentially quantified intermediate sequence, with the remark that reasoning about refusals may be omitted because chaining cannot introduce deadlock.
