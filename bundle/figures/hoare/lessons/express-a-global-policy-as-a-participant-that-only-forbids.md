# Express a global policy as an extra participant that adds no actions of its own and can therefore only forbid

**Lesson:** A ring of identical parties, each acquiring two shared items before it can do useful work, will eventually seize up with every party holding one item and waiting for another. The instinctive repairs all modify the parties, and each is expensive in its own way. Make one of them asymmetric and you have destroyed their interchangeability and acquired the problem of everyone agreeing which one is special. Impose a global acquisition order and the policy is now smeared across every participant, so revising it means revising all of them and any newcomer who has not read the rule breaks it. Buy more of the contended item and you have paid money to avoid thinking. There is a fourth move that is cheaper than all three: introduce one further participant whose vocabulary consists entirely of actions the existing parties already perform, and which contributes no action of its own. Having nothing to offer, it can only withhold; taking part in the actions of many parties, it can withhold combinations that no single party is in a position to see.

The properties that make this safe deserve naming, because they are what to verify before believing any such addition. The new party's vocabulary is contained in the system's, so the space of joint configurations cannot grow: it cannot introduce behaviour, only prune it, and therefore cannot invalidate any prohibition already established about the system. Every existing party's description is unchanged, so each one's individual argument survives verbatim and the parties stay interchangeable and separately developable. And the policy now exists in exactly one object, usually a small counter with a ceiling, so revising it is an edit in one place rather than an audit of everybody. Compare that with the ordering discipline, where the policy exists only as a convention distributed across participants who cannot check each other.

What the move does not give you free is the guarantee you actually wanted. A participant that only subtracts can never make anything happen, and progress is exactly the kind of property that composition does not preserve, so freedom from seizing up has to be proved for the assembly as a whole — typically as a case analysis over the situations the new bound still permits, showing that in each one somebody can move. That work is the price, and it is unavoidable by any means. The general shape to take away: when a policy is about combinations of local actions — how many may be in a phase at once, which two must never coincide, who may proceed while another is mid-operation — resist compiling it into each participant, and look for a way to state it as one more party that joins in the relevant actions and offers none. Admission control, ticket counting and mutual exclusion are all this shape. The test that tells you whether you have it is containment of vocabularies: if the new party needs actions of its own, it is not a constraint but a component, and none of the reasoning above applies to it.
