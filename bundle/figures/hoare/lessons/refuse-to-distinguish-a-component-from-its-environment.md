# Refuse to distinguish a component from its environment, and make the joining operator closed, symmetric and associative

**Lesson:** In most designs a part, an assembly and the surrounding world are three different kinds of object. The part has an interface; the assembly has a topology; the environment is a source of stimuli described informally, or not described at all. Collapse the three. Describe the surroundings with exactly the apparatus you use for the part, and define joining so that combining two of these things yields a third thing of the same kind. Everything sayable about a part is then sayable about an assembly and about the entire world, and words like component, system and context stop naming categories of object and start naming positions in an expression. The payoff arrives immediately: any analysis you develop applies at every scale including scales not yet built, because there is nothing distinctive about the outermost bracket. It also removes the recurring embarrassment of having no way to write down what the environment is allowed to do, which is where most integration surprises actually originate.

Three algebraic properties are what make the collapse real rather than nominal, and each is a concrete freedom. Symmetry of the joining operator says neither party is privileged — no observer and observed, no driver and driven — so every argument made about one side is available for the other by swapping names, and half your reasoning is free. Associativity says the bracketing of an assembly is not a decision, so you may regroup an existing system to suit one argument and regroup it differently for the next without re-deriving anything, which is what makes a large system analyzable in pieces of your own choosing rather than the pieces the builder happened to use. A unit — a party that constrains nothing — lets participants be added and removed without special cases in notation or proof. Without these three you still have a composition mechanism, but the claim that everything is the same kind of thing is decoration, and the arrangement will turn out to matter in ways you find out about one at a time.

The cost is that the homogeneous vocabulary forbids saying several things that feel natural: that this is inside that, that a stimulus comes from outside, that one piece is passive and another drives. Where such distinctions are genuinely present they have to be re-expressed in the shared terms, as facts about what each party is capable of, and that re-expression is real work. It is usually worth doing, because those distinctions are far more often inherited from how the thing happens to be deployed than from the problem itself, and they are precisely the assumptions that shatter when a system is reused at a different scale or when a component everyone treated as a leaf grows an interior. A design that never encoded the distinction has nothing to break when it stops being true.

**Source:** [Communicating Sequential Processes](../works/communicating-sequential-processes-book.md) — the introduction to the concurrency chapter, which argues that the environment of a process may itself be described as a process, that the complete system composed of a process and its environment is again a process definable from its components and placeable within a wider environment, and that it is best to forget the distinction between processes, environments and systems altogether; together with the laws of the following section establishing the symmetry of parallel composition as the logical symmetry between a process and its environment, its associativity as the irrelevance of assembly order, and the always-willing process as its unit.
