# Define "better" as "more predictable", and let implementation be a walk up that order

**Lesson:** You need a relation saying when one thing is an acceptable substitute for another — when a concrete artifact honours an abstract description. Rather than inventing a bespoke conformance check, put an ordering on the descriptions themselves, in which one sits above another exactly when it is less able to misbehave: fewer circumstances in which it can go wrong, fewer in which it can decline something wanted. Implementing then means moving upward. An abstract description sits below every artifact that honours it, and the correctness question becomes an instance of a comparison you already possess laws for rather than a separate apparatus. The payoff arrives when the combining operations turn out to be monotone with respect to that order, because monotonicity is exactly the statement that improving a part can never degrade the whole — which is what makes it sound for two teams to work on two components without consulting each other.

The bottom of the order must be chosen deliberately, and the obvious candidate is wrong. It is tempting to place the inert do-nothing thing at the bottom because it seems the least of all things, but under this ordering the inert thing is highly predictable and therefore sits rather high. The genuine bottom is the object that promises nothing whatever: unconstrained in what it may do and equally unconstrained in what it may refuse. That is what "no information yet" actually means, and it has to be an element of the domain, because resolving self-referential definitions works by starting at the bottom and climbing. Notice that this makes the choice of ordering and the choice of starting point a single decision rather than two — change what counts as better and the starting point moves with it, and a scheme that keeps the old starting point after changing the ordering will quietly compute the wrong thing.

What you buy is that several notions people usually keep apart collapse into one relation and stop needing separate treatment. Deliberate underspecification is being lower. Unpredictability is being lower. An implementation decision is any step upward. A description with many acceptable realizations is a point with many things above it, and "the most permissive description consistent with these requirements" becomes a least-upper-bound question with an actual answer. The habit worth carrying: whenever a project has a conformance notion, and separately an abstraction notion, and separately a notion of behaviour left unspecified, ask whether all three are one ordering wearing three names. If they are, unifying them deletes rules and hands you theorems in their place.

**Source:** [Communicating Sequential Processes](../works/communicating-sequential-processes-book.md) — the mathematical-theory section closing the nondeterminism chapter, which defines a process by its alphabet, failures and divergences; orders processes so that one is above another when its failures and divergences are contained in the other's, glossing this as being less likely to diverge and less likely to fail, and hence more predictable and more controllable; establishes the chaotic process as the least element of that order; defines the limit of a descending chain by intersection; and defines recursion by iterating from the chaotic process rather than from the stopped one, the change of starting point following directly from the change of ordering.
