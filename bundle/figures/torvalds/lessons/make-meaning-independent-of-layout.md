# Make meaning independent of layout, and layout becomes a free variable you can spend entirely on the machine

**Lesson:** Git's storage design has two layers that are usually fused. The conceptual layer knows only whole, immutable objects: no notion of a delta, no notion of an ordering, no idea that anything is compressed. The physical layer packs those objects into a single file, compresses them, and represents most of them as differences against other objects, using an elaborate pile of heuristics to decide which object to difference against which. The crucial property is that the upper layer's meaning does not depend on any decision the lower layer makes. Every possible packing order produces a correct repository. The heuristics can be badly tuned, arbitrary, or replaced wholesale, and nothing above them can observe the difference except in space and time.

That firewall is what makes the aggressive optimization affordable. Once being wrong is impossible, you are free to be crude: sort objects by a hash of the trailing path component so that similarly-named files across directories cluster together, difference within a fixed-size sliding window because a larger window costs more comparisons than it saves bytes, cap the difference chain depth at a small round number, and skip pairs a cheap size test says will not pay off. None of those choices has to be justified from first principles, and none can be defended as optimal — they only have to be better than nothing. The designer's own account of why the system stayed stable points at exactly this: because the basic ideas never grew a delta concept, the bugs that occur are simple bugs, and a bug that gets an object-store detail wrong is usually too obvious to survive.

With correctness insensitive to layout, layout can be chosen purely for the hardware. Objects are written in order of reachability from the current tip, so the bytes a reader is most likely to want are physically adjacent at the front of the file, which is a decision about disk and cache behavior and nothing else. Differences are taken from larger objects toward smaller ones, which is both denser — deleting a byte range costs almost nothing to encode while inserting one costs the inserted bytes — and, given that files tend to grow over time, means the reconstruction chains point backward into history rather than forward, keeping recent data cheap to read. Compression is applied per object rather than across the whole file, deliberately giving up compression ratio to preserve random access. Every one of these is a trade made against a physical cost model, and every one is safe to revisit later, because none of them means anything.

A programmer who believes this looks, before optimizing, for the seam that makes optimization non-load-bearing — and if there isn't one, builds it first. They keep the conceptual model free of the artifacts of its own efficiency, because a model that has to explain deltas, orderings, or compression to its users has permanently spent cognitive budget on something that should have been invisible. And once the seam exists, they feel entitled to choose physical layout by measurement and hunch, not by proof.

**Source:** [Git Version Control System](../works/git-version-control-system.md) — the packing-heuristics discussion preserved in the project's technical documentation, where the designer walks through the two independent orderings (one for locality of reading, one for clustering likely-similar objects), states outright that any order yields a working pack and that the ordering matters only for how well it clumps, explains why differences run from larger to smaller objects and why compression is per-object, and credits the model's stability to never exposing deltas at the conceptual level.
