description: A late essay arguing that computing science is fundamentally a branch of mathematics concerned with formal manipulation of uninterpreted symbols, and that treating programs as objects to be understood by running them (rather than by proving properties about them) is a pedagogical dead end. Despite the title, most of the piece is a broader argument about how radically computers changed what kind of intellectual discipline is possible, with the difficulty of actually teaching that discipline addressed only toward the end. It's Dijkstra at his most polemical and least willing to compromise on the formal-methods position he'd been building since the 1960s.
