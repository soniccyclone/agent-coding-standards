description: A brief note showing that moving to a formal system of strictly higher logical type can shrink the shortest available proof of some statements by more than any computable function of the original proof length — proofs don't just get shorter, the speedup is unbounded and non-recursive. This is an early, purely logical ancestor of the idea that the choice of formal system (or, later, machine model) can matter enormously for the resources a proof or computation requires. It anticipates questions computational complexity theory would take up formally decades later, about how proof or computation length varies across different systems of representation.
