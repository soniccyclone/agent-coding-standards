# Encode a system's own text as data inside the system, and questions about it become computations in it

**Lesson:** The move that unlocks everything here is deciding that the symbols, the well-formed expressions, and the derivations of a formal system are just finite structures, and that finite structures can be injected into whatever domain the system already talks about. Once each sign gets a number, each sequence of signs the number built from its members' numbers, and each sequence of sequences the same treatment one level up, the whole apparatus of "what counts as a formula," "what counts as a step," "what counts as a derivation" stops being commentary from outside and becomes arithmetic the system itself can state. Properties of the notation turn into properties of numbers; operations on notation — concatenation, bracketing, substituting for a variable — turn into arithmetic functions. The encoding is doing exactly what a data representation does in a program: it moves something from the meta-level, where you can only talk about it, to the object level, where you can compute with it.

Two things make this hold rather than merely sound clever. First, the encoding must be *faithful and invertible*: distinct texts get distinct codes, and from a code you can recover the text. Unique prime factorization is what buys that here, but the general requirement is the one any serializer faces — the representation has to lose nothing you intend to reason about. Second, the encoding must be *effective in both directions*: the predicates over codes that mirror the syntactic notions have to be ones you can actually evaluate, or the translation buys you nothing. Gödel's long ladder of definitions exists to establish precisely this, that each syntactic notion he needs corresponds to a computable numeric relation, built up from the previous ones.

The programmer who has internalized this stops treating "the program" and "the data" as different kinds of thing. Compilers, interpreters, macro systems, serializers, self-hosting toolchains, reflective runtimes, and analyzers over syntax trees are all the same move: pick a representation of your own artifacts inside your own value domain, define the predicates you care about over that representation, and let ordinary computation answer questions that used to require a human standing outside. The corollary is a design discipline as well: the quality of the reification is the quality of everything built on it, so the encoding of a language's syntax is not plumbing, it is the load-bearing decision. And the discipline cuts the other way too — a system with no faithful internal representation of its own artifacts cannot analyze, optimize, or extend itself, no matter how expressive it is in other respects.

**Source:** [On Formally Undecidable Propositions of Principia Mathematica and Related Systems I](../works/on-formally-undecidable-propositions.md) — the numbering of basic signs and the injection of sign-sequences and sequences-of-sequences into the naturals, followed by the long sequence of definitions that recasts each syntactic notion as a numeric relation. Also [On Undecidable Propositions of Formal Mathematical Systems](../works/on-undecidable-propositions-of-formal-mathematical-systems.md) — the section that renumbers a leaner system from scratch and the following one, which draws the explicit conclusion that part of the theory *about* the system thereby becomes statable *within* it.
