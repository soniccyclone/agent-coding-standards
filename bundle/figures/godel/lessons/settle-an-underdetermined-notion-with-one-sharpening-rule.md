# When a notion is left vague, add the one rule that makes it definite and let the contested cases fall out as consequences

**Lesson:** The four disputed statements Gödel handles in this note look independent. They concern selection, the sizes of infinite collections, and the measurability and structure of particular sets of reals — different questions that different specialists had been arguing over separately. He does not settle them separately. He isolates a single sharpening claim about what the objects of the theory *are*, shows that claim is safe to adopt, and then all four disputed statements come out as derivations from it. His own remark on why this is a good move is the important part: the base theory left the central notion vague, and the added claim's virtue is that it makes the vague notion definite. The disputes were symptoms of underspecification, so one decision at the point of vagueness cleared all of them at once.

This is the correct response to a family of arguments that will not converge. When the same design discussion recurs in different disguises — which of two conflicting behaviours is right at this edge case, does this operation see writes from that one, what is the order here, what happens on an empty collection, is this identifier the same identifier — the recurrence is evidence that some notion in the design was never pinned down and every team is filling the gap with its own reading. Adjudicating case by case produces a rulebook of unrelated decisions, each defensible and none derivable from anything, which is the worst possible outcome for anyone trying to reason about the system later. The move that pays is to go find the underdetermined notion and make one commitment there, chosen so the contested cases become consequences rather than policies. Canonical forms, a stated memory model, a definite identity criterion for records, a decided evaluation order: each is one added rule that retires a large collection of arguments.

Two constraints keep this from becoming a licence to decide things arbitrarily. First, Gödel earns the right to add his claim before he uses it, by the relative-safety construction; a sharpening rule that contradicts something the system already guarantees is not a clarification, it is a breaking change wearing a clarification's clothes. Second, the payoff has to be checked, not assumed: the rule is worth its cost only if the questions you actually cared about really do follow from it. A rule that makes the notion definite but leaves the disputes open has added a primitive and bought nothing. The pair of obligations — show it is consistent with what exists, show the open questions become derivable — is what separates a genuine sharpening from a preference asserted loudly.

**Source:** [The Consistency of the Axiom of Choice and of the Generalized Continuum-Hypothesis](../works/consistency-of-the-axiom-of-choice-and-generalized-continuum-hypothesis.md) — the structure of the result, in which the four propositions are obtained as deductions from the single added claim about the nature of every set, together with the closing remark arguing that this claim completes the theory naturally because it renders a previously indefinite notion definite. The derivation of the disputed statements from the single sharpening claim is carried out in detail in [The Consistency of the Continuum Hypothesis](../works/the-consistency-of-the-continuum-hypothesis.md) — its final chapter, which obtains selection immediately from a canonical choice of least-order element and then spends the remainder deriving the size statement.
