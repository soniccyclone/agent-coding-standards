# The strongest check on a restricted world is that running its own construction inside it reproduces exactly itself

**Lesson:** After showing that his restricted world obeys all the theory's axioms, Gödel has a further obligation that is easy to miss and is the keystone of the whole argument. The construction that produced the restricted world is itself a definition in the theory. So the inhabitants of that world can run it, and what they get is whatever their own version of the construction produces. If they got something smaller, the statement he wants to hold there — that everything is reachable by the construction — would be false inside, even though it is true from outside. What he proves is that the two agree: the construction carried out within the restricted world yields precisely the restricted world. That closure is what makes the extra axiom hold internally rather than merely from a privileged external vantage point, and everything else he wants follows from that axiom holding internally.

The reason this is a strong check rather than a formality is that it is not automatic and it is not cheap. Getting it requires that every ingredient of the construction be faithful under reinterpretation, which is exactly why the preceding audit had to cover the well-ordering, the indexing machinery, the recursion, and each generating operation. The internal-versus-external gap this closes is the standard source of confusion in reasoning about nested systems: a property can be true of a sub-world without being true in it, and only the second kind is available to anything living inside. Gödel keeps the two apart in his notation and never conflates them, and the payoff is that the final consistency claim is a claim about derivations in the theory rather than about a picture in the author's head.

The engineering analogue is the self-hosting or idempotence check, and it is the highest-value single test for any subsetting mechanism. A compiler that compiles itself and reproduces its own binary has demonstrated something no test suite demonstrates. A formatter or normalizer whose output is a fixed point has proved that its notion of normal form is stable. A sandbox whose own policy engine runs correctly inside the sandbox has shown its restrictions are expressible in the restricted environment. A configuration reducer that, applied to its own output, changes nothing has shown its reduction is complete. Each of these replaces an unbounded audit with a single decisive experiment, because passing it requires every internal component to be faithful, and any component that is not shows up as a discrepancy. The habit worth taking is to look, for each restricted environment you build, for the statement of the form "running the restriction inside the restricted thing gives the restricted thing back," and to treat failure of that statement as evidence the boundary is wrong rather than as an acceptable quirk.

**Source:** [The Consistency of the Continuum Hypothesis](../works/the-consistency-of-the-continuum-hypothesis.md) — the chapter devoted to establishing that the proposition identifying every set with a constructible one holds inside the model, which turns on proving that the class of constructible sets is absolute, i.e. that relativizing the whole generating apparatus to the model returns the same class, and the closing argument converting that into a transferable consistency statement about derivations.
