# Before grinding harder, check whether your technique could ever reach the conclusion — a method blind to the distinction you want cannot find it

**Lesson:** There is a class of results that says nothing about the object under study and everything about the tools being pointed at it. Take the family of arguments that proved the earliest impossibility results, the ones that construct a paradoxical object by tabulating machine behavior against itself. Those arguments have a structural property: they continue to go through unchanged when every machine involved is granted access to an arbitrary external answer source. So if there is even one such source under which the distinction you are trying to draw collapses, no argument with that property can draw it, and the collapse has been exhibited. The technique is not weak here; it is provably blind. Effort spent refining it is wasted no matter how much of it you spend.

The second barrier is subtler and shows the same reasoning turned reflexive. Nearly every method that has succeeded at proving size lower bounds on restricted circuit families shares a family resemblance: it works by exhibiting a property that separates the target function from most functions while itself being cheap to detect. If cryptographically strong pseudorandom generators exist, no property of that shape can succeed against unrestricted circuits, because a property both broadly applicable and cheaply detectable is exactly what such a generator defeats. The strange loop is that a successful proof of the shape would itself constitute a faster factoring algorithm than anyone has. The obstacle and the goal are made of the same material, and recognizing that is worth more than another decade of attempts.

The generalizable move is to characterize the reach of your method as sharply as you characterize your problem, and to look for a scenario your method cannot distinguish from the one you are in. If such a scenario exists and the conclusion is false in it, stop. This is an argument shape available at every scale of engineering. A test suite that would pass identically whether or not the defect is present cannot find the defect, and no amount of running it changes that. A monitoring signal indifferent to the failure mode you fear will never report it. Auditing the discriminating power of an instrument, rather than its thoroughness, is the cheapest useful step in most investigations.

The complementary discipline is knowing what to do once the barrier is established. When the direct result is out of reach, the substitute is a statement of the form "this cannot be easy unless everything in its class is," which is not the theorem you wanted but carries most of the practical weight and is provable today. Naming the honest surrogate is part of the same intellectual hygiene as naming the barrier.
