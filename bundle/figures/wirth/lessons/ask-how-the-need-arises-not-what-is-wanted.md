# Ask how the need arises, not what the user wants, then answer it with a restricted construct

**Lesson:** When users of an abstraction report that it is insufficient, they will name the mechanism they already know how to get the job done with, and that mechanism will almost always be something from the layer you were trying to rise above — unrestricted transfer of control, values with no type, direct manipulation of addresses. Answering the request as stated imports the layer's hazards wholesale and undoes the abstraction for everybody, including the people who never needed the escape. The productive question is not what facility they want but how the situation arises that made them want it. That question has answers of a completely different shape: not a jump, but a way to end a repetition on a condition rather than on a counter running out; not an address, but a way to refer to a structure created during execution; not an untyped word, but relief from performing bit-level encoding by hand in order to store several small pieces of information compactly. Each of those is satisfiable without leaving the level of abstraction you are defending.

The remedies that work are deliberately weaker than what was asked for, and the weakness is the feature. A restricted loop whose termination is a condition covers the real need, and buys something the unrestricted version cannot: the behavior of the composite construct follows from the behavior of its body alone, so a rule for reasoning about the whole from the part actually exists — which is impossible once control can leave or, worse, re-enter from outside. Pointers become tractable under three restrictions taken together: each is confined to referring to one type of object so checking survives; they may refer only to things with no declared name of their own, so the aliasing problem never arises; and reference versus referent is always written explicitly, so there are no silent conversions to misread. And a record of named, individually typed components, laid out densely, delivers exactly what the hand-packed word delivered while keeping every part checkable by compiler and reader alike. Note that this third one makes the language larger and the compiler harder, and is still correct: added volume is not the same thing as added conceptual complexity, and a facility can pay for its bulk with the incoherence it prevents.

Two habits generalize out of this. First, the acceptable form of a new capability is one that can be explained entirely within the system's own vocabulary — the disqualifying property is not being unsafe but requiring an appeal to the underlying machine in order to be understood at all. Anything that can only be explained by what the hardware does has punched a hole through every guarantee above it. Second, expect the cost of the restrictions to be visible and the benefit to be invisible, and to be argued at accordingly. The checking is a tax you can point at; the confidence in the resulting artifact is diffuse and shows up as problems that never happened. Being able to say that the tax was repaid many times over is a judgment based on having lived with both, and it is the judgment worth developing, since it is what the argument always comes down to.

**Source:** [On the Design of Programming Languages](../works/on-the-design-of-programming-languages.md) — the "Three Examples Revisited" section: the injunction to ask how the programmer's problem arises rather than what he wants because the answer to the second will always be jumps, type-less operands and addresses; the replacement of the goto by while and repeat forms whose total effect is deducible from the repeated component; the three security measures for pointers (bound to a single type, referring only to anonymous variables, explicit dereferencing); the packed record with named typed components replacing hand-done octal encoding, with the note that it complicates the compiler and increases the language's volume without reducing its conceptual simplicity; and the closing observation that these restrictive rules repaid their cost in confidence.
