# Examine the degenerate instance, and when you re-represent it keep only the distinction that mattered

**Lesson:** A parameterized design has a smallest instance, and it is usually dismissed on the grounds that it is useless for the purpose the parameter was introduced to serve. That dismissal is correct and incomplete. The smallest instance is useless *for that purpose*; the reason the parameter existed was to match a cost that came from a particular setting, and outside that setting the reason evaporates while the structural rules remain. So the productive question is not whether the degenerate instance is good at the original job but what it becomes when you take the original job away — and the answer is often a structure with its own strong properties, discovered for free by asking a question that costs nothing. Make it a habit to instantiate every design parameter at its extremes and look at what falls out, especially the extreme everyone skips.

Changing setting also invalidates the representation, and this is where the second half of the discipline applies. A layout chosen because transfers were expensive and units were large — a fixed block with slots, sized for the medium — is exactly wrong when the units become tiny and space is the scarce resource. Re-representing means asking what the abstraction was actually contributing, separately from how it was stored. Very often the storage form can be dissolved into the surrounding structure entirely: the grouping disappears, its members become ordinary elements of the host structure, and everything the grouping was doing collapses into a single distinction — which of an element's links leads to another member of the same former group, and which leads out of it. One bit per element carries what a whole nested container was carrying.

The move is worth naming because it generalizes far past this case: when an abstraction is expensive to keep as a distinct runtime entity, find the minimum information that its existence was conveying and store that instead. The test of whether you have found the minimum is that every operation on the old form can be rewritten in terms of the surviving distinction, with no case that needs to ask a question the bit cannot answer. If such a case exists, you dissolved too much. And notice the trade being made: the dissolved form is more economical and strictly harder to reason about, since a structure that used to be visible as one object is now implied by a predicate spread over many. Take the trade when space genuinely binds, and write down what the bit means, because nothing else in the representation will say it.

**Source:** [Algorithms and Data Structures](../works/algorithms-and-data-structures.md) — section 4.7.2's treatment of the first-order case of the multiway scheme, opening with the remark that the seemingly least interesting species is sometimes worth attention, noting that it is useless for large indexed data sets on secondary stores because roughly half the pages would hold a single item, and therefore setting secondary storage aside and reconsidering the structure for a single-level store; and the same section's consequent redesign of the node representation, rejecting the array of items as unsuitable when optimal storage economy is mandatory, replacing it with a short linked list, then merging the descendant pointers with the item-list pointers so that the page loses its identity and the items themselves become the nodes of an ordinary binary tree, with a single Boolean per node recording whether its right link is horizontal — that is, whether it leads to a former page-mate rather than to a descendant.
