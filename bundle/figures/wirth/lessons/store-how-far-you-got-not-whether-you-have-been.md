# Store how far you got, not whether you have been

**Lesson:** A traversal that must be able to leave an element and come back to it needs some record at that element, and the instinctive record is a flag: have I been here. A flag is the wrong choice, and noticing why generalizes well beyond traversals. What the algorithm actually needs on return is not the fact of having visited but the point at which to resume — which of the element's several outgoing directions to take next. Store that instead, as a count of directions already taken, and the flag comes free: a count of zero means untouched, anything else means in progress or finished, and the comparison against the element's out-degree distinguishes those two. One field, chosen to answer the harder question, answers the easier one as a side effect.

The payoff is structural rather than a saved byte. An element with a resume index can be left and re-entered any number of times without keeping anything outside it, which is what allows a traversal of a branching structure to be suspended at every level with no stack at all — the per-element state that a stack frame would have held is exactly this index, and it has been distributed into the elements themselves. Try to build the same thing on a boolean and you find that returning to an element tells you nothing about what to do next, and the missing information has to be reconstructed or stored elsewhere, which is where the auxiliary structure comes back. That is the general shape: when a scheme needs external bookkeeping, look at what the per-item state actually has to answer, and check whether you have been storing a weaker fact than the algorithm requires.

Two extensions come almost free once the field holds a count. The condition governing whether to descend can be strengthened by any additional test on the candidate — most usefully, that it is itself untouched, which is a check on the same field — and the traversal then works on structures with shared parts and cycles rather than only on trees, without a separate visited-set. And a third state can be encoded in the same field by reserving a disjoint range of its values, so a scan can distinguish untouched, touched, and a category the traversal never produces at all. Before adding a parallel structure to record a property, check whether the field you already have can be widened to carry it: a field with three ranges is usually cheaper and always more local than a field plus a table.

**Source:** [Project Oberon](../works/project-oberon.md) — section 8.3's generalization of the pointer-inverting traversal from binary trees to nodes of arbitrary degree, where the mark value, starting at zero for unmarked, is used as a counter of descendants already traversed and hence as the index of the descendant to be processed next, with the loop comparing that index against the node's number of descendants to decide between descending and returning; the note that the algorithm applies not only to trees but to arbitrary structures including circular ones if the continuation condition is extended to also require that the candidate descendant be unmarked, causing already-marked descendants to be skipped; the constraint imposed by the field's width, that no record may contain more than a fixed number of reference fields; and the scan phase's use of the same field with a negative range to mark an element free, giving the three cases of unmarked, marked and free with their respective actions of collecting, unmarking, and skipping or merging.
