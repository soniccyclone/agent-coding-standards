# A well-solved subproblem is not a licence for complexity, and rigor is an instrument, not a deliverable

**Lesson:** When one part of a problem develops a deep, general theory, the complexity that part used to deter stops being deterred — and this is a failure mode rather than a gain, because the theory removed the cost from one participant's ledger and not from anyone else's. Knowing that a mechanical procedure can be produced for essentially any structure you invent makes structural complexity feel free, so it gets admitted freely. What that reasoning silently assumes is that the only reader who mattered was the machine. A structure that is straightforward for a program to take apart is also straightforward for a person to take apart, and the converse holds too; the same property serves both, so there was never a reason to spend it. Committing to a structure analyzable by the simplest method with minimal lookahead is therefore not a concession to implementation weakness but a decision about who is going to be reading. Before accepting complexity anywhere on the grounds that a known technique handles it, ask which participant the technique actually relieved, and check whether the ones it did not relieve are the ones doing the work.

The same misallocation shows up in what a field chooses to teach and study. Depth of theory accumulates where problems are tractable and formalizable, not where they are hard, so the amount of theory around a subproblem is a poor indicator of that subproblem's share of the real difficulty. Recognizing well-formed input is the part with the elegant theory; coping with input that is wrong — locating the mistake, reporting it usefully, and continuing in a way that yields further information rather than noise — is the part that consumes the effort and determines whether the thing is usable. That reorders the design priorities, because diagnosability is a property of the language being processed and not only of the processor: a structure chosen for ease of analysis is also a structure in which a mistake can be localized and described. And the genuine prerequisite for building the processor turns out to be general competence at constructing large programs and choosing methods for their parts, not mastery of the branch with the deepest results.

Formal definition deserves the same audit, since it too is a capability that invites misuse by its own success. It is a real instrument: attempting to state a design in a mathematical formalism exposes hidden inconsistencies and forces out notions that cannot actually be explained in terms of the abstractions you claim to be building on, which is a test worth running precisely because it fails informatively. But it is an instrument for the designer, not a document for the user, and it is not an end in itself. Rigor contributes volume, and volume is not what someone trying to accomplish a task needs; a formal definition cannot substitute for an informal presentation and teaching material, and treating the existence of a formalism as evidence that the design is now adequately communicated confuses having checked something with having explained it. Keep the two products distinct and be clear about which audience each one serves.

**Source:** [On the Design of Programming Languages](../works/on-the-design-of-programming-languages.md) — the "Language Design is Compiler Construction" section: the argument that deep parsing theory misled designers into treating syntactic complexity as free since a parsing algorithm could always be found, the observation that a language simple to parse for a compiler is also simple to parse for a programmer, the claim that the real challenge in compiling is coping with erroneous programs rather than recognizing correct sentential forms, and the point that experience developing large complex programs is the essential prerequisite; plus the concluding recommendations to adhere to a syntax analyzable by recursive descent with one-symbol lookahead as an aid to diagnosis, to use a mathematical formalism to detect hidden inconsistencies and eliminate notions not explicable in terms of the chosen abstractions, and not to treat a formal definition as an end in itself or as a substitute for tutorial material.
