# Sharing is only observable under update, which is exactly why it has to be sayable

**Lesson:** Whether two positions in a structure hold the same thing or two equal copies of it is a distinction with no consequences at all as long as nobody writes. Every read returns identical answers either way, every traversal produces the same output, and any test you can perform passes identically on both. The moment selective update enters, the distinction becomes the most important fact about the structure, because a change made through one position either appears at the other or does not, and no amount of inspecting the values will tell you in advance which. This is the general shape of the problem: identity is invisible under observation and decisive under mutation. It follows that a design cannot defer the question until it bites — by then the representation has already answered it, possibly by accident.

The practical consequence is a constraint on notation. A system that treats references as an invisible implementation technique, allocating and linking behind your back, has quietly removed your ability to express intent about them. It will either duplicate what you meant to share or share what you meant to duplicate, and in neither case can you write down which you wanted; the language has no word for it. Making the reference an explicit value of its own type restores that vocabulary, and the cost — a notational distinction between a datum and a designation of a datum — is small next to the cost of a program whose aliasing is settled by an allocator's convenience. The rule generalizes past pointers: whenever a property is invisible to every operation your system currently supports but would become critical under an operation you might later support, spend the notation now, because you cannot retrofit a distinction into code that was written without the ability to make it.

There is a matching obligation on the reference itself. An unconstrained address can designate anything and therefore tells you nothing about what you will find at the other end; a reference bound at declaration to the type of what it designates carries that information into every use, and the redundancy between the declaration and the use is what lets a checker catch the mistakes. That redundancy is the whole difference between a designation you can reason about and a raw machine address, and it is worth paying for it in exactly the same way and for exactly the same reason: the checkable statement of intent is what a reference costs to be safe.

**Source:** [Algorithms and Data Structures](../works/algorithms-and-data-structures.md) — section 4.2's argument that an implementation hiding the concept of pointers or using a different storage technique would force the ancestor records of the shared grandparents to be represented twice, that in accessing their data for inspection it does not matter whether they are duplicated or represented by a single record, that the difference is essential when selective updating is permitted, and that treating pointers as explicit data items rather than as hidden implementation aids allows the programmer to express where storage sharing is intended and where it is not; together with the same section's statement that a pointer type is bound to the type of the elements it points to and that this binding is what distinguishes pointers in higher-level languages from addresses in assembly codes, increasing security through redundancy of the notation.
