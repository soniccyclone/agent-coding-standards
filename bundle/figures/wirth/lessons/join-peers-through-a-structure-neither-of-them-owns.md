# Join peers through a structure neither of them owns

**Lesson:** When one component must hand work to another, the obvious connection is a call: the producer names the consumer and invokes it. That single decision imports far more than a procedure. It makes the producer depend on the consumer's existence, its readiness, its speed, and its failures — the producer cannot be built, tested, or reasoned about without the consumer being present and working, and if the consumer is slow or unavailable the producer is stuck holding data it has no way to put down. Both components have then been welded into a single unit of development and a single unit of failure, which is exactly what the decomposition was meant to prevent.

The alternative is to introduce a third thing that is only a data structure with operations on it, and to have both components depend on that instead of on each other. The producer deposits; the consumer withdraws; neither names the other, and the dependency graph now has two arrows into a leaf rather than one arrow between two peers. Three properties follow immediately and they are the whole reason to do it. Development becomes genuinely parallel, because either side can be exercised against the structure alone. Timing is decoupled, because the deposit completes without regard to when the withdrawal happens — which is the actual requirement whenever the two activities have different urgency, different reliability, or different availability. And extension becomes addition: a new participant is anything that can deposit into or withdraw from the same structure, so it slots in without a single existing module being edited or even recompiled.

Two things determine whether this pays off. The intermediate must be genuinely inert — a structure and its operators, with no policy, no knowledge of what the entries mean, and above all no dependency of its own on the participants, or it becomes a hub through which the coupling has merely been routed. And each entry deposited must be self-sufficient: it has to carry both what is to be done and the material to do it with, because by the time it is withdrawn the depositor is gone and cannot be consulted. That second requirement is the real design work, and it is worth doing carefully, since an entry that is complete makes the consumer's job a pure function of what it withdrew, and an entry that is not silently reintroduces the coupling you paid the intermediate to remove. Where durability matters, the entry should reference something that outlives both parties, so the handover survives the failure of either.

**Source:** [Project Oberon](../works/project-oberon.md) — section 11.1's account of the server system's structure, in which data received for printing or for dispatch into mailboxes is stored into temporary files by the network server and then handed over to the print or mail server rather than passed by procedure call, on the stated grounds that reliability and timing made the direct solution unattractive and that a weaker coupling in time between data transmission and data consumption is desirable; the description of this data-centred interface, contrasted with a procedural one, as making the individual servers independent in the sense that none imports any other so their development could proceed autonomously, with their connection being a module defining a first-in-first-out queue and its operators whose elements are called tasks because each carries both an objective and the file to be processed; and the accompanying remark on the ease with which a further server is inserted into the scheme as an additional source and sink for the same queues.
