description: The formal definition of Pascal, describing a small language of strict, statically-checked data types (records, arrays, sets, enumerations, pointers) built as a teaching-oriented successor to Algol 60. Wirth designed it explicitly around a compact set of orthogonal constructs rather than an accumulation of features, arguing that a language's power should come from how its parts combine rather than from how many parts it has. Pascal went on to become the dominant teaching language of the following two decades and the direct ancestor of Modula-2 and Oberon.
