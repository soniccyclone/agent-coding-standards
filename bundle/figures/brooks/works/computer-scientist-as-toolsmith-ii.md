description: Brooks's Allen Newell Award address argues that computer science's most valuable output is often not new theory but well-built tools — compilers, graphics systems, simulation environments — that let researchers in other disciplines see and solve problems they couldn't approach before. He frames toolsmithing as a legitimate first-class intellectual activity rather than a lesser, purely applied cousin of "real" computer science, drawing on his own work in molecular graphics and virtual environments at UNC as evidence. The talk pushes back on a science-envy tendency in the field to value theorem-proving over instrument-building, arguing the discipline's engineering side deserves equal standing.
