# Insist on running the construction, and the constants you were hiding become numbers

**Lesson:** For decades the constructions in this area lived at the level of asserting that a suitable machine exists. Chaitin refused to leave them there. He designed a small functional language, wrote its interpreter more than once in different host languages, wrote the universal machine in that language, wrote a compiler that turns machine programs into arithmetic, and cross-checked two independent implementations against each other on a large suite of cases. The point was not tidiness. Two things fall out of executing a construction that cannot be obtained by describing one.

The first is that the vague constant becomes a specific quantity, because the constant in these theorems is literally the length of a program, and once the program is written the number can be counted. A bound with a number in it can be compared to another bound, argued about, and improved. A bound with an unexamined constant in it is a place where you may be concealing an enormous cost from yourself, and nobody can tell, including you. The second is that running the thing forces the details a sketch elides: what happens on malformed input, how many failure modes there really are, how much information the interface actually needs to be handed. Every one of Chaitin's language design decisions was extracted from him by the attempt to run something.

His epistemological claim is blunter than the methodological one: you understand something only if you can program it, and program it yourself rather than reading someone else's. Reading a construction gives you the feeling of understanding and not the substance, because the feeling is generated by the parts you did follow and the substance is in the parts you skipped. For a programmer this argues for building the smallest executable version of any design you claim to understand, and for treating an unquantified constant, an unmeasured overhead, or an untested edge as a marker of where your understanding actually stops.
