description: Introduces a public-key encryption and signature scheme (now known as the Rabin cryptosystem) whose security reduces provably to the difficulty of factoring the public modulus, rather than resting on an unproven hardness assumption the way RSA does. Shows that inverting the function for even a small fraction of inputs is enough to factor the modulus, giving a tight equivalence between breaking the scheme and factoring. One of the earliest public-key proposals with a genuine security proof.
