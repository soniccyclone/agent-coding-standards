description: A close reading of cuneiform mathematical tablets (roughly 1800-1600 BCE) as step-by-step procedures rather than as isolated arithmetic results, treating Babylonian scribal instructions as genuine algorithms in the modern sense — sequences of operations meant to be followed mechanically to solve a class of problems. Knuth provides English translations and commentary tying the tablet procedures to recognizable algorithmic structure (iteration, substitution) millennia before the term existed. It's as much a piece of history-of-mathematics scholarship as it is computer science, and it underpins his broader claim that algorithmic thinking predates computers by thousands of years.
