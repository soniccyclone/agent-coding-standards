description: A short SIGACT News piece arguing that computer science had been sloppily overloading O-notation to mean both "upper bound" and "exact order," and proposing the now-standard trio of O (upper bound), Omega (lower bound), and Theta (tight bound) to say precisely which one is meant. It's a notational-hygiene paper more than a technical result, but the vocabulary it proposed is the one every algorithms textbook now teaches as settled convention. Knuth traces the tangled prior usage of the symbols back through analytic number theory to make the case for cleaning it up.
