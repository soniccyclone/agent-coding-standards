# Design so that the method's inability to improve the answer is itself the proof the answer is best, checkable without rerunning the method

**Lesson:** An optimization routine that stops and reports an answer leaves you with a trust problem: to believe the answer is optimal you must believe the routine, which usually means rereading it. Edmonds arranges things so that the stuck state carries a second object of a completely different kind, and that object bounds the problem from the other side. When the search for an improvement exhausts itself, the exhausted structure can be read directly as a covering family whose total capacity equals the size of the solution found. Since any such family obviously bounds any solution from above, equality settles optimality on the spot. The dead end is the certificate. In the weighted versions the certificate is a vector of numbers attached to elements and to sets of elements, and checking it means verifying a handful of local inequalities plus equality on the chosen elements, with no reference to how the vector was obtained.

The habit of thinking this way pays three ways. It gives you a checkable answer: the certificate can be validated by a much simpler, independently written procedure, so a bug in the clever method produces a detectable failure rather than a plausible wrong answer. It gives you a theorem: the fact that the two quantities always meet is a min-max statement about the problem, a generalization of the classical duality result for the easy special case, and generalizations of that kind are how a problem's structure gets discovered rather than merely exploited. And it gives you a stopping rule with no arbitrariness in it, because "no improvement is available" now has a positive witness rather than being the absence of one.

The design discipline this implies is to construct the dual object alongside the primal one throughout, not to derive it afterwards. Both papers that carry weights do exactly this: the numbers accompanying vertices and sets are maintained and adjusted as the search proceeds, and the adjustment rules are chosen to keep the certificate conditions true at every step. The certificate is thus an invariant of the running method rather than a postscript, which is also why it can be produced at no extra asymptotic cost. A programmer who works this way asks, of any procedure claiming to find a best-anything, what witness the procedure will hand over, and treats the absence of a candidate witness as evidence that the problem is not yet understood.

**Source:** [Paths, Trees, and Flowers](../works/paths-trees-and-flowers.md) — the duality section, where the stuck tree is converted into a minimum-capacity covering family and the maximum-matching-equals-minimum-cover theorem is proved by induction on the number of unmatched vertices. Also [Maximum Matching and a Polyhedron with 0,1-Vertices](../works/maximum-matching-and-a-polyhedron-with-0-1-vertices.md), whose dual vector and its accompanying equality conditions form a necessary and sufficient test for optimality, and [Optimum Branchings](../works/optimum-branchings.md), which constructs the dual vector by induction backwards through the sequence of collapsed graphs and illustrates it as a separate figure guaranteeing the answer. The minimum-cost section of [Theoretical Improvements in Algorithmic Efficiency for Network Flow Problems](../works/theoretical-improvements-in-algorithmic-efficiency-for-network-flow-problems.md) states the same pattern most compactly: cost-optimality of a partial answer is characterized as the existence of a per-node numbering satisfying local inequalities, and the method keeps answer and numbering mutually consistent at every step rather than deriving the second at the end.
