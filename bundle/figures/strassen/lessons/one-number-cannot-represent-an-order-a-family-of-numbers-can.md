# One number cannot represent an order; a family of numbers can represent it exactly

**Lesson:** Summarize a comparison relation by a single numeric measure and you have thrown away most of it. The measure gives you one implication — if one object sits below another then its number is no larger — and the converse fails everywhere, so the number can refute a comparison but almost never confirm one. The repair is not a better number. It is to look for a whole family of numeric measures, each individually monotone with respect to the relation and each behaving like a homomorphism with respect to however objects combine, chosen so that the relation is recovered exactly: one object sits below another precisely when every member of the family says so. Now the family is not a summary of the order, it *is* the order, re-presented as pointwise comparison of functions. The measures you started with reappear as extremes of the family, one as its maximum and its mirror-image as its minimum, which explains at a stroke why they always looked like a maximum and a minimum should look.

What this buys is that qualitative questions about the relation become quantitative questions about a space. The family, topologized so that evaluation at each object is continuous, is a compact space of points; each object becomes a continuous function on it; and every question of the form "is this reachable from that" becomes "does this function dominate that one everywhere." Facts that were hard to see in the relation become one-liners: a cancellation law, for instance, is immediate once comparison is pointwise arithmetic, because you may divide by a nonvanishing function. And the whole family, taken as a single object, is a data structure that answers every comparison question at once. Determining it completely is then equivalent to settling every open problem in the theory, which reframes those problems as questions about the shape of one geometric object rather than an unbounded list of unrelated inequalities.

The transferable design instruction is to distrust the single scalar. When you have a preorder on configurations and a cost function that only weakly reflects it, ask what family of cost functions would reflect it exactly, and require of each candidate exactly the two structural properties the representation needs — monotone in the order, and compatible with composition and combination. Even a partial family is progress, because each member is an independent necessary condition, so each one you find rules out comparisons the old single measure could not. And the moment the family is complete, incomparability stops being a gap in your theory and becomes a visible fact: two objects are incomparable exactly when two members of the family disagree about them, and you can point at which ones.

**Source:** [The Asymptotic Spectrum of Tensors](../works/the-asymptotic-spectrum-of-tensors.md) — the introduction and section 3: a compact Hausdorff space of order-monotone ring homomorphisms is associated with a set of bilinear maps, each object is mapped to a continuous real function on it, the asymptotic preorder is shown to hold exactly when the corresponding functions dominate pointwise, asymptotic rank and asymptotic subrank are identified as the maximum and minimum of an object's function, a cancellation law is derived immediately from the representation, and the paper notes both that effective knowledge of the space would settle all questions about the order and that the space can be read as a structure recording all possible degenerations.
