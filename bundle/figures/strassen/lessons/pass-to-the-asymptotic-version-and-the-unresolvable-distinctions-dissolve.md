# Pass to the asymptotic version of a relation: distinctions you could not settle dissolve, and the algebra you needed appears

**Lesson:** Suppose you are stuck with two competing definitions of the same intuitive relation. One is exact and strict, the other admits limits and approximations, they provably differ, and no argument settles which is the right primitive. Suppose further that the exact relation refuses to acquire the algebraic properties you want — you can prove it cannot be extended to the ambient structure in a way compatible with addition and multiplication, so there is no hope of manipulating it the way you manipulate numbers. Both problems can be dissolved by the same move: define the asymptotic version of the relation. One object stands below another asymptotically when high powers of the first are dominated by high powers of the second together with a factor that grows subexponentially. Because the tolerance is subexponential, it is invisible to anything that grows exponentially, and both of the original relations have the same asymptotic version. The distinction you could not resolve was never asymptotically real, so the choice you were agonizing over stops being a choice.

The second payoff is that the relaxed relation has structure the exact one could not carry. The asymptotic version does extend compatibly to the whole ambient structure; it supports cancellation, so you can divide out a common factor; it is insensitive to enlarging the ground environment in ways the exact relation was not; and it lands, verifiably, in a class of preordered structures for which someone else has already built a representation theory. Each of those is something the exact relation demonstrably lacked. The pattern is worth naming: an exact relation that is too rigid to be algebra, and a relaxed relation that is well behaved because it has quotiented out precisely the sub-exponential noise that made the rigid version brittle.

Two cautions come with the move. First, the asymptotic relation is coarser, so you have deliberately given up the ability to distinguish things that differ by a bounded or slowly-growing amount, and any question genuinely about constants has to be asked before you relax. Second, the coarsening only helps if the tolerance you allow is *negligible against the scale of the quantities you compare*; pick a tolerance of the same order as the thing being measured and you have not built a robust relation, you have built a vacuous one. The right tolerance is the largest one that is still invisible at the growth rate you care about, and choosing it correctly is what makes the resulting notion independent of accounting conventions — of which operations you decided to count, and of the exact model you were working in.

**Source:** [The Asymptotic Spectrum of Tensors](../works/the-asymptotic-spectrum-of-tensors.md) — the introduction's definition of the asymptotic preorder by domination of high powers up to a subexponential factor, together with its remark that the exponent's asymptotic definition is robust against the choice of computation model; the observation that the exact restriction order admits no compatible positive cone in the ring while the asymptotic one does, and the cancellation law that follows; section 3's theorem that restriction and degeneration have the same associated asymptotic preorder, so the choice between them is immaterial asymptotically; the corollary identifying the asymptotic preorder as the coarsest well-behaved preorder refining the original; and the theorem that the resulting spectrum is unchanged by ground field extension.
