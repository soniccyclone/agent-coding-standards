# The obvious next generalization of a sound system is where soundness usually dies — attack it with the classic paradox before you build on it

**Lesson:** Systems that work invite a particular move: notice the pattern that makes them work, apply it one level further up, and expect the properties to come along. The move is seductive because the generalization is genuinely natural — nothing about it looks reckless, the extension is described in a paragraph, and it removes an arbitrary-seeming restriction. It is also where a large fraction of sound systems die. Allowing a system to talk about a collection that includes the very thing you are constructing is exactly the shape of every classical paradox, and dressing that shape in modern type-theoretic notation does not defuse it. The discipline is to treat a proposed generalization as a hostile object until proven otherwise, and the most efficient hostile act available is to try to reproduce a known paradox inside it. Those constructions are ancient, short, and reusable; if your extension admits one, you find out in an afternoon rather than after a year of building on sand.

Two refinements make this more than caution. The first is to *localize*. Having found an inconsistency, do not stop at "the extension is bad" — determine the minimal set of features that already suffices to derive it. That answer is what tells you which of your candidate designs are salvageable: here the offending argument survives in a fragment using only a handful of constructs, and separately, a version of the system without the self-referential quantification is conjectured to be fine. Knowing exactly which feature carries the poison converts a total loss into a boundary you can design against, and it is also what lets you evaluate other people's systems quickly — the same inconsistency immediately settled the status of a contemporary rival design.

The second refinement is that a derived contradiction is a reusable instrument, not a tombstone. Once you have a system you have proved cannot be consistent, anything that would let you build it becomes something that cannot exist. That inference chain gets used here to remarkable effect: because the speculative system is contradictory, no model of the well-behaved system can contain a single equality test usable at every instantiation, since such a test would let you construct the contradictory system inside the model. The paradox is what converts an open question about what features are achievable into a proof that a specific feature is unachievable in principle — and impossibility results of that kind are the most valuable output a foundational investigation produces, because they stop other people from searching.

For a working engineer the transfer is direct. When extending a system that currently has a property you depend on — termination, determinism, an invariant, a security boundary — do not ask whether the extension is convenient. Ask what the extension now lets a component say about itself or about the collection it belongs to, and construct the self-referential case deliberately. Then keep the broken version around: it becomes your proof that certain features must never be added.

**Source:** [Interprétation fonctionnelle et élimination des coupures de l'arithmétique d'ordre supérieur](../works/interpretation-fonctionnelle-et-elimination-des-coupures.md) — the appendix presenting a system offered explicitly as a natural generalization of the type-theoretic setting and then shown contradictory by reconstructing the classical order-type paradox inside it, including the identification of a minimal offending fragment and the note that a restricted variant may survive; and the following appendix, which uses that contradiction to prove no extensional model can contain a universal equality test.
