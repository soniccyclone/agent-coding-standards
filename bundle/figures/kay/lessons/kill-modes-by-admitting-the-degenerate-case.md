# Remove a mode by generalizing the representation until the competing operations become one, usually by admitting the degenerate case

**Lesson:** "Modeless" is worthless as a slogan and valuable as an operational property, so state it operationally first: from wherever someone currently is, they can begin the next thing they intend without first backing out of the current thing. Specialized contexts are not the problem — a region of a system can be devoted to one kind of work — the problem is a required exit ritual standing between a person and their next intent. Stated that way the property becomes auditable, and it stops being about interfaces. A library that demands teardown before a different call sequence is legal, a build that requires a clean before it will believe you, a protocol with a session you must close before opening another: each of these fails the same test, and each imposes the same tax, which is that the user must hold in mind where they are and what it forbids. It is also worth noticing that discovering one place where the property already holds is what makes the rest of a system look intolerable by comparison; that contrast is the cheapest source of design standards you will get.

The wrong way to remove a mode is to add an escape from it. The right way is to find the generalization in which the competing modes are the same operation evaluated at different points, and the move that most often produces it is admitting a degenerate case the representation currently forbids. Let a selection have zero width and inserting, deleting and replacing stop being three operations with three sets of rules: everything is replacement, and the old distinctions reappear as boundary values of the arguments rather than as branches in the code or states in the user's head. The cost was nothing but the willingness to let a value be empty. This generalizes far past text: a great deal of mode proliferation and special-case branching traces back to refusing empty collections, zero-length ranges, absent-but-valid values, or a unit element for some composition. Wherever you find a cluster of operations that are almost the same, look for the value your types are currently refusing to admit.

Two habits make this practical. First, look for the worst modes in the small, unglamorous corner everyone considers solved, because that is where accumulated conventions are least examined and where "it has always worked this way" does the most damage. Second, when you find the generalization, expect not to be alone: a simplification that is genuinely forced by the structure of the problem tends to be found by several people at nearly the same time, which is confirmation that the generalization was real rather than clever, and a reason to go looking for whoever already has it instead of defending priority.

**Source:** [User Interface: A Personal View](../works/user-interface-a-personal-view.md) — the definition of modeless as always being able to reach the next thing without backing out, arrived at from overlapping windows activated by pointing rather than by explicit switching, the resulting campaign to remove modes everywhere after contrast with conventional command syntax, and the account of eliminating insert, replace and delete as separate text-editing modes by allowing selections between characters so a zero-width selection makes every operation a replacement — a solution several people reached independently at the same time.
