# Find the one mechanism your whole stack is secretly made of, and pick the element that stays meaningful at every level

**Lesson:** Layers of a system are conventionally built by different specialists with unrelated vocabularies, so the fact that they are doing the same thing goes unnoticed. Look again at what each layer actually performs and the coincidences are startling: recognizing structure in a stream and acting on what was recognized describes instruction selection in a code generator, the handling of a network protocol, forward and backward inference, the definition of every language in the system, and a user's search over their own data. If that many layers are instances of one abstraction, the correct response is not to implement the abstraction five times at five levels of quality but to build one engine for it that is strong enough to be the implementation everywhere. The engine then becomes the thing worth investing in, because every improvement to it lands simultaneously in places that had no apparent connection, and the layers stop needing translation machinery between their incompatible intermediate forms.

Restating a subsystem as an instance of your general mechanism is a design move in its own right, and it is worth trying even when the subsystem has a settled conventional shape. A protocol implementation reconceived as recognition-plus-repair rather than as a state machine with buffers collapses, and the collapse is not compression — the resulting text is closer to what the protocol's own specification says, so it is easier to check against the specification than the conventional version was. That is the signature of a good restatement: the artifact gets smaller and more obviously right at the same time. If it gets smaller but harder to justify, the mechanism was forced onto a problem it does not fit, and the honest move is to leave the two apart.

The same discipline applies to choosing the primitive element a subsystem is built from, and here there is a criterion that is easy to miss. Efficiency pressure pushes toward whatever the lowest layer wants, which for graphics means the shape hardware rasterizes most readily. But the element also has to carry meaning upward, all the way to the person using the system, or every level above the bottom is forced to reintroduce its own concepts and the unification is lost. Prefer the element that is directly nameable by a user and still cheap to realize, and then insist that everything is made of it — including the things that traditionally get their own subsystem, such as text. The payoff is that a whole category of machinery, the toolkits and widget hierarchies that exist only to reconcile different kinds of visible thing, ceases to have a reason to exist.

**Source:** [STEPS Toward the Reinvention of Programming](../works/steps-toward-the-reinvention-of-programming.md) — the plan-of-attack section identifying pattern matching and transformation as one abstraction recurring from code generation up to end-user scripting, the tiny network-stack work reconceiving a protocol as non-deterministic recognition with error heuristics, the interpreter obtained by hanging side effects off a grammar, and the universal-polygon section arguing for choosing the graphical element that is meaningful to users at every scale and building even glyphs from it, which removes the usual multiplicity of interface components.
