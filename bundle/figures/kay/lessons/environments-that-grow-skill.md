# An environment people get good in offers early subgoals, many stances, no penalty for being wrong, and compounding results

**Lesson:** When people fail to become competent with a tool, the usual explanation is that they lacked persistence, and it is usually wrong. Four properties of the environment predict whether skill accumulates, and each one is a design decision rather than a fact about the user. The first is early subgoals: something real and satisfying must be achievable long before mastery, so practice pays out continuously instead of being an investment against a distant promised return. Tools that require substantial competence before producing any result the user cares about will be abandoned by people who would have become excellent at them, and no amount of documentation compensates, because the missing thing is a reason to continue rather than an explanation of how.

The second is plurality of stance. Sustained engagement is not sustained attention on one thing; it is the freedom to keep changing relationship to the same material — to make something, then judge it, then take it apart, then compete with it, then explain it. What looks like a short attention span is usually a system that permits exactly one role and exhausts it. A system that lets a person move between building, inspecting, measuring and playing will hold them far longer than one that is equally interesting but admits only one way of being present.

The third is safety: there must be regions where being wrong costs nothing, neither in damaged state nor in exposure. Serious testing against reality has its place, but if every action is consequential, users stop trying things, and trying things is the entire mechanism by which the model in their head gets corrected. The fourth is compounding: what you produce must become usable material for what you do next, so effort accumulates instead of evaporating at the end of each task. An environment where yesterday's result is inert has silently reset every user to novice each morning. Fast, forgiving feedback ties all four together, since it is what makes an attempt cheap enough to be worth making.

**Source:** [A Personal Computer for Children of All Ages](../works/a-personal-computer-for-children-of-all-ages.md) — the argument that a finished picture is a satisfying subgoal reachable without mastery while conventional instrument practice withholds any such subgoal for months, the reframing of short attention span as difficulty remaining in a single role toward an idea, and the characterization of a good environment as safe and covert, productive in the sense that what is learned feeds further learning, and immediately responsive.
