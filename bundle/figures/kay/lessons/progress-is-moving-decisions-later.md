# Progress is the history of moving decisions later, and generality costs nothing if the common case runs full speed and only the exception traps

**Lesson:** Read the sequence of accepted advances in computing as one process and a single pattern appears: in each case something that had been fixed at construction time was moved to a later moment. Programs and their parameters were wired, then became changeable storage. Address arithmetic was performed by rewriting the instructions, then deferred to registers consulted at execution. Where a computation lives, which parameters a procedure has, how much memory a structure occupies and when it is reclaimed — each was once decided early and each was, at some point and against resistance, moved later. Seen this way, the design question in front of you is rarely "which mechanism" and usually "how late can this decision be made without becoming unaffordable," and the family of designs that historically won are the ones that pushed it latest. The corollary is that a system organized around deferring as many decisions as possible is not one style among several; it is the accumulation of that whole sequence applied at once — to what a component contains, where it is, what it is called, when and why it acts.

The objection is always efficiency, and it is almost always answered the same way, so the answer is worth learning as a technique rather than rediscovering per case. Deferring a decision does not require paying for the deferral on every operation. Arrange the common case to execute at the speed it would have had with no generality at all, with a cheap concurrent check that diverts to the general path only when the operands are not the ordinary ones. The general machinery then costs nothing in the case that dominates, and the cases where it costs something are the ones that could not have been handled at all under the fixed scheme. Better still, look for an existing mechanism whose overhead has already been accepted and hide the dispatch inside it — a lookup that the system performs anyway can often be made to carry the extra discrimination for free.

The last part of the pattern is uncomfortable and is the one designers most often decline. Hardware is not a given that software must accommodate; it is a set of decisions that were made earlier and frozen, usually to make the previous generation of programs run well. Treating it as fixed means letting an old set of software assumptions dictate what your program designs are allowed to be, and it is how excessive local optimization comes to stand in for progress. The historically effective posture has been to build the deferred mechanism anyway, wear the inefficiency, demonstrate what it buys, and expect the substrate to be revised afterwards — which has repeatedly taken years and happened anyway.

**Source:** [The Early History of Smalltalk](../works/the-early-history-of-smalltalk.md) — the closing section, which reads the succession of advances from stored programs through index registers, relocation, memory management and recursion as a single history of finding ways to bind later and then persuading manufacturers to support them, characterizes the object scheme as late binding applied comprehensively, argues that hardware is merely software frozen early and that treating it as given has held back program design, and describes the fast-path-plus-trap arrangement in which ordinary arithmetic proceeds at full speed under a cheap check while the general dispatch is folded into address translation the machine already performs.
