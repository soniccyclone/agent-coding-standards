description: Kay's doctoral dissertation proposes the FLEX machine, an early personal-computer design pairing simple hardware with an extensible, interactive programming language meant for non-expert users. It works through interpreter design, data typing via coercions, and a "kernel language" whose own definitional facilities extend it, prefiguring the message-passing and late-binding ideas Kay later built into Smalltalk. It is the earliest full documentation of his personal-computing ideas, predating "A Personal Computer for Children of All Ages" by three years.
