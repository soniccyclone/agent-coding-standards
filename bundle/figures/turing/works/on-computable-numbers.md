description: Defines computability by describing an idealized machine that reads, writes, and moves along an infinite tape one step at a time, rather than by a syntactic calculus of substitution rules. Turing uses this machine to show that no general procedure can decide, for an arbitrary such machine, whether it ever halts, which settles Hilbert's Entscheidungsproblem in the negative by a route independent of Church's lambda calculus. It is the founding text of the mechanism-first lineage in computability theory, later shown equivalent to Church's approach.
