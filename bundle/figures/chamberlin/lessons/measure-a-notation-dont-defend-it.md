# Learnability is a measurable property of a notation, so measure it

**Lesson:** Arguments about whether a syntax is easy are usually arguments about taste, and taste produces no actionable list. The alternative is to treat ease as an experimental question with a subject population and an outcome variable: teach the notation to people who do not already know it, watch where they fail, and record which specific construct produced each failure. Done this way the result is not a verdict on the language but a defect list naming individual features, and a defect list can be worked. The order of events matters here — a first version was published, then run through controlled trials with untrained learners, and the trials isolated particular constructs as sources of difficulty; the revision that followed changed exactly those constructs rather than rewriting to a new intuition.

The same empiricism applies before any design exists. A prior measurement of what real programs in an existing language actually contained — the finding that the overwhelming majority of statements are simple — is cited as a design input, and it licenses a specific asymmetry: optimize the surface for the common simple case even at the cost of making the rare hard case verbose, because the distribution of use, not the distribution of expressible forms, is what determines aggregate difficulty. Designers who reason instead from the hardest expressible query end up making every user pay for machinery almost nobody needs. And after shipping, the third measurement is field use: a multi-year evaluation at real installations yielded another concrete list — a shorthand for existence testing, partial-match string comparison, a way to plan a statement once and run it many times with different values, and an operator preserving unmatched rows across a combination. Each of these is a gap that no amount of desk reasoning surfaced, and each was closed because someone had gone looking for gaps with users rather than with intuition.

A programmer who has absorbed this stops treating interface quality as unfalsifiable. Before defending an API's shape, they find someone who has never seen it, give them a task, and watch; they instrument which constructs actually appear in the code their users write, and reallocate the syntax budget accordingly; and they keep a channel open for the requests that reveal missing vocabulary rather than dismissing them as user error. The discipline is cheap and the payoff is specificity: measurement tells you which feature to change, which no amount of principled argument can.

**Source:** [SEQUEL: A Structured English Query Language](../works/sequel-a-structured-english-query-language.md) — the introduction's appeal to a published empirical study of statement complexity in existing programs as justification for optimizing the simple case, and its framing of a target population of non-specialist professionals. Also [SEQUEL 2: A Unified Approach](../works/sequel-2-a-unified-approach.md) (the introduction's account of human-factors trials with students that isolated specific features as learning obstacles and drove the revision) and [A History and Evaluation of System R](../works/a-history-and-evaluation-of-system-r.md) (the language-evaluation portion of the field-use phase, which reports both the learnability observation and the numbered list of user-requested language additions).
