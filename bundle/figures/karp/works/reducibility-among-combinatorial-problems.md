description: Building directly on Cook's 1971 theorem about Boolean satisfiability, Karp shows that 21 well-known combinatorial and graph problems (vertex cover, clique, Hamiltonian circuit, integer programming, and others) are all polynomial-time reducible to one another and hence NP-complete. The paper's real contribution is methodological — it lays out the reduction technique researchers still use today to classify a new problem as intractable — rather than any single result about one problem. It turned NP-completeness from a curiosity about one artificial problem into an organizing principle for the whole field of algorithm design.
