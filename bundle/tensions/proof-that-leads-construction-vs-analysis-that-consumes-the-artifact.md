# Where in the workflow the correctness argument attaches

## The decision
You are choosing how assurance work is placed against the work of producing the artifact. Dijkstra places it in front: the postcondition is the object you start from, guards are computed rather than invented, and the specification drags the code into being. Sifakis names that exact method, agrees it is the better intellectual discipline, and tells you to choose the other one, because a technique welded to construction cannot be automated, handed to a second person, postponed, or cut short when the date arrives. His arrangement puts a seam in the process: one group produces a description, another runs checks on it, in parallel.

## Dijkstra: the obligations should exist while the constructions are still choosable
[Work backwards from what must be true at the end](../figures/dijkstra/lessons/let-the-proof-lead-the-program.md) is an argument about cost, not about purity. Proving a finished program doubles the work, because the reasoning has to be reconstructed against a text that was written without it. Deriving instead makes construction goal-directed: for each candidate statement you compute the condition under which it delivers the goal, that condition becomes its guard, and what remains uncovered tells you precisely which branches you still owe. The method is also honest about where it stops being mechanical, since choosing the invariant and the decreasing quantity is invention and everything after it is bookkeeping, and knowing which is which tells a programmer where to spend attention. The closing move matters for this tension and is easy to miss: the claim is explicitly not that programs should be manufactured by calculation, but that a programmer should always know what would have to be proved and should favour constructions whose obligations are visible while there is still time to choose a different construction.

## Sifakis: a method that shares a schedule line with production loses to one that does not
[A method that must be practiced while building competes with building](../figures/sifakis/lessons/separate-the-analysis-from-the-act-of-building.md) is a diagnosis of why the better discipline did not spread, and the reasons are structural rather than moral. An entangled proof is charged to the same person, in the same hours, against the same deadline as the artifact. It cannot be given to somebody who did not write the code, and it has no partial state: either the argument closes or you are holding nothing. An analysis that consumes a finished description can be run by another group, on another schedule, and yields real evidence at whatever depth it reached when the calendar ran out. The instruction that follows is to evaluate a technique by placement rather than by strength, asking whose time it spends, whether it is transferable, and what falls out of it if you stop halfway, because a weaker guarantee that fits an organisation's division of labour does more good than a stronger one requiring the organisation to be rebuilt.

## Resolution
**LLM DECISION — Nathan may overturn.**

Sifakis is not objecting to the argument leading the construction; he is objecting to the argument being made per program. Once that is the variable, both instructions hold, and Sifakis has already written the bridge himself.

[Heavy reliance on after-the-fact checking is a symptom of a discipline that lacks construction rules](../figures/sifakis/lessons/put-the-guarantee-in-the-construction-rule.md) says that an argument establishing a property for components with stated local guarantees wired in a stated shape can be read in either direction, as a check on an existing system or as a constraint obeyed while building one, and that read the second way it costs nothing at analysis time because the artifact was never allowed into the bad region. That is Dijkstra's arrangement exactly, with one difference: the argument was made once, about a pattern, by somebody else. Per-pattern arguments are automatable, delegable and reusable, which are the three properties Sifakis says entangled proof lacks. Per-program derivation of a novel invariant is none of those things and never will be, which is why Dijkstra himself locates the invention there and the grinding everywhere else. The two lessons are not competing methods; they are the same discipline at two granularities, and the mapping between them is that anything you derive twice should stop being a derivation and become an assembly law.

The second half of the dissolve concerns feedback rather than delegation. Sifakis's picture of analysis consuming a description it did not influence is not something he actually recommends, since the same lesson tells you that checkability is a design property to aim at and that an architecture landing outside the region where your analyses stay cheap has bought a recurring verification bill no tool will refund. So the analysis does shape the artifact; it shapes it one level up, in the choice of architecture, rather than statement by statement. What Dijkstra requires is that the verdict can still change the thing being judged. What Sifakis rejects is a shared schedule line. Milner shows those are separable, and shows it with evidence rather than argument: [specify and build at the same time](../figures/milner/lessons/specify-and-build-at-the-same-time.md) records a design attacked simultaneously by a draft formal account, two independent implementations, a second semantics written in a different style, and a thesis, run by different people, each finding what the others could not, all of it early enough that the design changed in response. That is Sifakis's seam and Dijkstra's timing at once, which is only paradoxical if you had assumed the correctness argument must be produced by the same hand and hour as the text.

The residue that does not dissolve is truncation. Sifakis says ship at the assurance level the deadline permitted, with the completed checks standing as real evidence, and Dijkstra's structural position is that a program nobody can argue about should not exist. There is no layer trick for that one, and the switching condition is consequence rather than method: where the failure is bounded and repairable, partial evidence beats the nothing you get from an argument that did not close, and where it is not, a deadline is not a licence, it is a reason to have built something smaller. Jones states the same allocation in [spend the dividend on safety, not ambition](../figures/jones/lessons/spend-the-dividend-of-rigour-on-safety-not-ambition.md), with the sharper observation that the parts amenable to proof and the parts likely to be wrong are often not the same parts, which is an argument for keeping both instruments rather than for ranking them.

**Strongest counter-argument:** the per-pattern reading may be a place the field has been trying to reach for forty years rather than a place a practitioner can stand today. Compositional assembly laws with stated guarantees are exactly what Sifakis says computing does not yet have, which is why he calls the reliance on inspecting finished artifacts a statement about the immaturity of the composition theory. If the delegable laws mostly do not exist for the systems people actually build, then on any given project the choice really is between per-program derivation and after-the-fact checking, my resolution names a third option that is not on the menu, and Sifakis's original verdict stands unmodified. Milner would not rescue it either, since the Standard ML effort was a small group of unusually strong people defining one language over several years, which is closer to the conditions Sifakis says an organisation cannot be rebuilt into than to a counterexample against him.
